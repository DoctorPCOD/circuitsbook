    1.

    Derive the Laplace transform of
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = 2~\textrm{e}^{-5t}~u(t)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{2}{s+5} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the Laplace transform describing the exponential function as well as the multiplication property.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Derive the Laplace transform of
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = \left[6~t~\textrm{e}^{-4t}+2~\textrm{e}^{-4t} + 5\right]~u(t)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{7s^2 + 54s + 80}{s(s^2 + 8s + 16)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the Laplace transforms describing the exponential function, exponential function times time, and constant.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{6}{(s+4)^2} + \frac{2}{(s+4)} + \frac{5}{s} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Multiply all terms so they share a common denominator, and simplify to derive \(F(s)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} F(s) \amp= \frac{6s + 2s(s+4) + 5(s+4)^2}{s(s+4)^2}\\ \amp= \frac{7s^2 + 54s + 80}{s(s^2 + 8s + 16)} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Derive the Laplace transform of
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = \textrm{e}^{-t}\left[2\cos(10t) + 4\sin(10t)\right]~u(t)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{2s+42}{s^2 + 2s + 101} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the Laplace transforms describing exponential functions multiplied by sine and cosine. Simplify to derive \(F(s)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} F(s) \amp= \frac{2(s+1)}{(s+1)^2+100} + \frac{40}{(s+1)^2+100}\\ \amp= \frac{2s+42}{s^2 + 2s + 101} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Derive \(V(s)\text{,}\) the Laplace transform of \(v(t)\text{.}\) The differential equation describing \(v(t)\) and the initial condition of the voltage are given below.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + 0.25v(t) \amp= 0\\ v(0) \amp= 3~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} V(s) = \frac{3}{s+0.25} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the differentiation property of the Laplace transform, then solve for \(V(s)\text{.}\) Base units are used in all calculations.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= sV(s) - v(0) + 0.25V(s)\\ \amp= sV(s) - 3 + 0.25V(s)\\ V(s) \amp= \frac{3}{s+0.25} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Derive the Laplace transform of \(i(t)\text{.}\) The differential equation describing \(i(t)\) and the initial conditions of the current are given below.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + 6\frac{di(t)}{dt} + 109i(t) \amp= 0\\ i(0) \amp= 10~\textrm{A}\\ i'(0) \amp= -5~\textrm{A/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{10s + 55}{s^2 + 6s + 109} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the differentiation property of the Laplace transform, then solve for \(I(s)\text{.}\) Base units are used in all calculations.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= s^2I(s) - si(0) - i'(0) + 6\left(sI(s) - i(0)\right) + 109I(s)\\ \amp= s^2I(s) - 10s + 5 + 6sI(s) - 60 + 109I(s)\\ 10s + 55 \amp= s^2I(s) + 6sI(s) + 109I(s)\\ I(s) \amp= \frac{10s + 55}{s^2 + 6s + 109} \end{align*}

    Inverse Laplace Transforms

    6.

    Derive \(f(t)\) by calculating the inverse Laplace transform of \(F(s)\text{,}\) given below. Identify the terms that contribute to the transient response, then identify the terms that contribute to the steady-state response.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{10}{s+10} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = 10~\textrm{e}^{-10t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The only term in \(f(t)\) represents the transient response. There are no steady-state terms.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the Laplace transform describing the exponential function as well as the multiplication property. Because the term is a decaying exponential, it contributes to the transient response. There are no steady-state terms.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    Derive \(f(t)\) by calculating the inverse Laplace transform of \(F(s)\text{,}\) given below. Identify the terms that contribute to the transient response, then identify the terms that contribute to the steady-state response.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{10s^2 + 10}{s(s^2+10s+25)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = \left[0.4 - 52~t~\textrm{e}^{-5t} + 9.6~\textrm{e}^{-5t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Terms representing the transient response:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{tr} = \left[- 52~t~\textrm{e}^{-5t} + 9.6~\textrm{e}^{-5t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Terms representing the steady-state response:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{ss} = 0.4~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Factor the denominator of \(F(s)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{10s^2 + 10}{s(s+5)^2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The partial fraction expansion will take the form shown below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{K_1}{s} + \frac{K_2}{(s+5)^2} + \frac{K_3}{(s+5)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    To calculate coefficient \(K_1\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s)\) and solve at \(s=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{(10s^2 + 10)(s)}{s(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=0} \amp= \frac{K_1(s)}{s}\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_2(s)}{(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_3(s)}{(s+5)(s)}\Big|_{s=0}\\ \frac{10s^2 + 10}{(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=0} \amp= K_1\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_2(s)}{(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_3(s)}{(s+5)(s)}\Big|_{s=0}\\ \frac{10(0)^2 + 10}{(0+5)^2} \amp= K_1 + \frac{K_2(0)}{(0+5)^2} + \frac{K_3(0)}{(0+5)(s)}\\ \frac{10}{(5)^2} \amp= K_1\\ K_1 \amp= \frac{10}{25}\\ \amp= 0.4 \end{align*}
    🔗
    To calculate coefficient \(K_2\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+5)^2\) and solve at \(s=-5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{(10s^2 + 10)(s+5)^2}{s(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=-5} \amp= \frac{K_1(s+5)^2}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} + \frac{K_2(s+5)^2}{(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+5)^2}{(s+5)}\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \frac{10s^2 + 10}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} \amp= \frac{K_1(s+5)^2}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} + K_2\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \amp+ K_3(s+5)\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \frac{10(-5)^2 + 10}{-5} \amp= \frac{K_1(0)^2}{-5} + K_2 + K_3(0)\\ \frac{10(25) + 10}{-5} \amp= K_2\\ K_2 \amp= -52 \end{align*}
    🔗
    To calculate coefficient \(K_3\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+5)^2\text{,}\) differentiate with respect to \(s\text{,}\) then solve at \(s=-5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{d}{ds}\frac{(10s^2 + 10)(s+5)^2}{s(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=-5} \amp= \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_1(s+5)^2}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} + \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_2(s+5)^2}{(s+5)^2}\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_3(s+5)^2}{(s+5)}\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{10s^2 + 10}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} \amp= \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_1(s+5)^2}{s}\Big|_{s=-5} + \frac{d}{ds}K_2\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}K_3(s+5)\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \left(10-\frac{10}{s^2}\right)\Big|_{s=-5} \amp= \left(\frac{K_1(s^2-25)}{s^2}\right)\Big|_{s=-5} + 0\Big|_{s=-5} + K_3\Big|_{s=-5}\\ \left(10-\frac{10}{(-5)^2}\right) \amp= \left(\frac{K_1((-5)^2-25)}{(-5)^2}\right) + K_3\\ 10 - \frac{10}{25} \amp= \frac{K_1(0)}{25} + K_3\\ K_3 \amp= 9.6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The expanded form of \(F(s)\) is given below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{0.4}{s} + \frac{-52}{(s+5)^2} + \frac{9.6}{(s+5)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Consult Table 11.1.1 to derive the time-domain function.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = \left[0.4 - 52~t~\textrm{e}^{-5t} + 9.6~\textrm{e}^{-5t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The steady-state solution is described by terms without decaying exponentials.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{ss} = 0.4~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The transient solution is described by terms with decaying exponentials.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{tr} = \left[- 52~t~\textrm{e}^{-5t} + 9.6~\textrm{e}^{-5t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗

    8.

    Derive \(f(t)\) by calculating the inverse Laplace transform of \(F(s)\text{,}\) given below. Identify the terms that contribute to the transient response, then identify the terms that contribute to the steady-state response.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{s^2+4}{(s^2+16)(s^2+5s+4)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = \left[0.11~\cos(4t) + 0.066~\sin(4t) + 0.098~\textrm{e}^{-t} - 0.208~\textrm{e}^{-4t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Terms representing the transient response:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{tr} = \left[0.098~\textrm{e}^{-t} - 0.208~\textrm{e}^{-4t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Terms representing the steady-state response:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} f_{ss} = \left[0.11~\cos(4t) + 0.066~\sin(4t)\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Factor the denominator of \(F(s)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{s^2+4}{(s+j4)(s-j4)(s+1)(s+4)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The partial fraction expansion will take the form shown below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} F(s) = \frac{K_1}{(s+j4)} + \frac{K_2}{(s-j4)} + \frac{K_3}{(s+1)} + \frac{K_4}{(s+4)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    To calculate \(K_1\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+j4)\) and solve at \(s=-j4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{(s^2+4)(s+j4)}{(s+j4)(s-j4)(s+1)(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-j4} \amp= \frac{K_1(s+j4)}{(s+j4)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+j4)}{(s-j4)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+j4)}{(s+1)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+j4)}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \frac{s^2+4}{(s-j4)(s+1)(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-j4} \amp= K_1\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+j4)}{(s-j4)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+j4)}{(s+1)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+j4)}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-j4}\\ \frac{(-j4)^2+4}{((-j4)-j4)((-j4)+1)((-j4)+4)} \amp= K_1 + \frac{K_2(0)}{-j8}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(0)}{1-j4} + \frac{K_4(0)}{4-j4}\\ \frac{-12}{-160 - j96} \amp= K_1\\ K_1 \amp= 0.0551 + j0.0331 \end{align*}
    🔗
    \(K_2\) is equal to the complex conjugate of \(K_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} K_2 = 0.0551 - j0.0331 \end{equation*}
    🔗
