    Section 2.3 Delta-Wye and Wye-Delta Transforms

    As mentioned previously, there are situations when a resistive circuit cannot be reduced using parallel and series combinations. In those cases, it may be possible to transform the resistor configuration to a functionally identical equivalent. After completing a transformation, series and parallel combinations may be possible. The two transformations that will be discussed in this book are delta-wye and wye-delta transforms.
    🔗
    A delta circuit arrangement is shown in Figure 2.3.1.
    🔗
    The resistance between any two nodes in a delta circuit are parallel and series combinations of those resistors, defined in (2.3.1), (2.3.2), and (2.3.3).
    🔗
    \begin{align} R_{ab} \amp = R_1//(R_2 + R_3)\tag{2.3.1}\\ R_{bc} \amp = R_2//(R_1 + R_3)\tag{2.3.2}\\ R_{ac} \amp = R_3//(R_1 + R_2)\tag{2.3.3} \end{align}
    🔗
    A wye circuit arrangement is shown in Figure 2.3.2. Note that it has one more node than a delta circuit.
    🔗
    The resistance between any two nodes in a wye circuit are series combinations of those resistors, defined in (2.3.4), (2.3.5), and (2.3.6).
    🔗
    \begin{align} R_{ab} \amp = R_a + R_b\tag{2.3.4}\\ R_{bc} \amp = R_b + R_c\tag{2.3.5}\\ R_{ac} \amp = R_a + R_c\tag{2.3.6} \end{align}
    🔗

    Subsection 2.3.1 Delta-Wye Transforms

    A delta-wye transform modifies a delta arrangement of resistors (Figure 2.3.1) and converts it into an equivalent wye arrangement (Figure 2.3.2).
    🔗
    The equivalent resistance between any two nodes of the wye circuit must be identical to the corresponding equivalent resistances of the delta circuit in order to make this transformation valid. (2.3.1) is set equal to (2.3.4); (2.3.2) is set equal to (2.3.5); and (2.3.3) is set equal to (2.3.6). Then, the terms \(R_a\text{,}\) \(R_b\text{,}\) and \(R_c\) are solved for in terms of the resistances \(R_1\text{,}\) \(R_2\text{,}\) and \(R_3\text{.}\) (The algebraic details of these derivations will not be shown in this book.)
    🔗
    The results are described in (2.3.7), (2.3.8), and (2.3.9). These equations govern the delta-wye transform.
    🔗
    \begin{align} R_a \amp = \frac{R_1R_3}{R_1+R_2+R_3}\tag{2.3.7}\\ R_b \amp = \frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2+R_3}\tag{2.3.8}\\ R_c \amp = \frac{R_2R_3}{R_1+R_2+R_3}\tag{2.3.9} \end{align}
    🔗

    Subsection 2.3.2 Wye-Delta Transforms

    A wye-delta transform modifies a wye arrangement of resistors (Figure 2.3.2) and converts it into an equivalent delta arrangement (Figure 2.3.1).
    🔗
    To ensure the two circuits are equivalent, (2.3.1) is set equal to (2.3.4); (2.3.2) is set equal to (2.3.5); and (2.3.3) is set equal to (2.3.6). Then, the terms \(R_1\text{,}\) \(R_2\text{,}\) and \(R_3\) are solved for in terms of resistances \(R_a\text{,}\) \(R_b\text{,}\) and \(R_c\text{.}\) (The algebraic details of these derivations will not be shown in this book.)
    🔗
    The results are given in (2.3.10), (2.3.11), and (2.3.12). These equations govern the wye-delta transform.
    🔗
    \begin{align} R_1 \amp= \frac{R_aR_b+R_bR_c+R_aR_c}{R_c}\tag{2.3.10}\\ R_2 \amp= \frac{R_aR_b+R_bR_c+R_aR_c}{R_a}\tag{2.3.11}\\ R_3 \amp= \frac{R_aR_b+R_bR_c+R_aR_c}{R_b}\tag{2.3.12} \end{align}
    🔗

    Example 2.3.11 Using a wye-delta transform to calculate equivalent resistance.

    🔗
