    Divided Feedback Op-Amp Circuits

    1.

    Calculate the output voltage and circuit gain of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.1.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= -3~\textrm{V}\\ G \amp= -6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The circuit is an inverting op-amp circuit. Apply (4.5.2) to calculate the gain.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G \amp= -\frac{6~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Apply (4.5.1) to calculate the output voltage.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= GV_{IN}\\ \amp= (-6)(0.5~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= -3~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Calculate the output voltage and circuit gain of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.2.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= 8~\textrm{V}\\ G \amp= 1.6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The circuit is a non-inverting op-amp circuit. Apply (4.5.3) to calculate the gain.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G \amp= 1 + \frac{30~\textrm{k}\Omega}{50~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The voltage at the non-inverting node must be calculated before using (4.5.1) to calculate the output voltage. The voltage at the non-inverting node is equal to the voltage dropped over the 10 Ω resistor, which can be calculated with Ohm’s law. (To obtain a unit of V, first convert the source current to A to multiply with Ω. Alternatively, convert the resistance to kΩ to multiply with mA.)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} V_P \amp= (10~\Omega)(0.5~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Now use (4.5.1) to calculate the output voltage.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= GV_P\\ \amp= (1.6)(5~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= 8~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Calculate the output voltage and source gains of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.3.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= -0.65~\textrm{V}\\ G_1 \amp= -5\\ G_2 \amp= -2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The circuit is an inverting summing amplifier circuit. Apply (4.5.5) to calculate the output voltage and each of the source gains. The unit of each source has been converted to V.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= -\left(\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)0.01~\textrm{V}-\left(\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)0.3~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= -0.05~\textrm{V} - 0.6~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= -0.65~\textrm{V}\\ G_1 \amp= -\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -5\\ G_2 \amp= -\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Calculate the circuit gain of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.4 as a function of \(x\text{.}\) The potentiometer has a total resistance of 500 Ω. Calculate the minimum and maximum possible values of the circuit gain. Use the variable \(x\) to describe the potentiometer resistance between the wiper and 100 Ω resistor.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} G \amp= 1 + \frac{500~\Omega - x}{100~\Omega + x}\\ G_{min} \amp= 1\\ G_{max} \amp= 6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The resistance of the potentiometer is 500 Ω. This means that the sum of the input resistance (resistance between the wiper and the 100 Ω resistor, which the question states to express as the variable \(x\)) and feedback resistance (resistance between the wiper and output of the op-amp) is equal to 500 Ω.
    🔗
    The circuit is a non-inverting op-amp circuit. Apply (4.5.3) to calculate the gain. The \(R_1\) value in (4.5.3) refers to the resistance between the inverting node and ground, which is equal to \(x + 100~\Omega\text{.}\) The \(R_2\) value in (4.5.3) refers to the feedback resistance (the resistance between the inverting node and the output), which is equal to \(500~\Omega - x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The gain of the circuit can now be calculated.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} G = 1 + \frac{500~\Omega - x}{100~\Omega + x} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The smallest possible circuit gain occurs when the feedback resistance is 0 Ω and the input resistance is 500 Ω. In other words, when \(x = 500~\Omega\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G_{min} \amp = 1 + \frac{500~\Omega - 500~\Omega}{100~\Omega + 500~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The largest possible circuit gain occurs when the feedback resistance is 500 Ω and the input resistance is 0 Ω. In other words, when \(x = 0~\Omega\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G_{min} \amp = 1 + \frac{500~\Omega - 0~\Omega}{100~\Omega + 0~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1 + 5\\ \amp= 6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Calculate the output voltage and source gains of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.5.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= 4~\textrm{V}\\ G_1 \amp= -2\\ G_2 \amp= 2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The circuit is a difference amplifier. To calculate the output voltage, apply (4.5.9).
    🔗
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= -\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}3~\textrm{V} + \left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega+1~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)5~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= -6~\textrm{V} + (3)(0.67)(5~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= -6~\textrm{V} + 10~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Apply (4.5.10) to calculate \(G_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G_1 \amp= -\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Apply (4.5.11) to calculate \(G_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G_2 \amp= \left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega+1~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (3)(0.67)\\ \amp= 2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Calculate the output voltage and circuit gain of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.6.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= 1.5~\textrm{V}\\ G \amp= 3 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    This circuit doesn’t fit any of the standard op-amp circuit templates (inverting, non-inverting, summing, or difference amplifier). Therefore, the assumptions of an op-amp circuit (no input current, virtual node) can be applied with any useful circuit laws and theorems to calculate these parameters.
    🔗
    Apply KCL at the inverting input. The voltage at the inverting input will be referred to as \(V_X\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{0-V_X}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{V_X-V_{OUT}}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ -V_X \amp= 0.5V_X - 0.5V_{OUT}\\ 0 \amp= 1.5V_X - 0.5V_{OUT} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Apply KCL at the non-inverting input. The voltage at the non-inverting will be equal to \(V_X\) due to the virtual node.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{0.5~\textrm{V} - V_X}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{V_X}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{V_X-V_{OUT}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 0.1~\textrm{V} - 0.2V_X \amp= 0.5V_X + 0.25V_X - 0.25V_{OUT}\\ 0.1~\textrm{V} \amp= 0.95V_X - 0.25V_{OUT} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(V_X\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(V_{OUT}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 \amp -0.5 \amp 0\\ 0.95 \amp -0.25 \amp 0.1\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0.5\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 1.5\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \(V_{OUT}\) is therefore equal to 1.5 V. Use (4.5.1) to calculate the circuit gain.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G \amp= \frac{1.5~\textrm{V}}{0.5~\textrm{V}}\\ \amp= 3 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    Calculate the output voltage and source gains of the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.7.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= 3.31~\textrm{V}\\ G_1 \amp= 1.69\\ G_2 \amp= -0.69 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Perform a source transformation on the current source to obtain a circuit with two voltage sources. The feedback resistors have also been re-drawn in a manner that might make the circuit analysis more intuitive to solve.
    🔗
    This circuit doesn’t fit any of the standard op-amp circuit templates (inverting, non-inverting, summing, or difference amplifier). Therefore, the assumptions of an op-amp circuit (no input current, virtual node) can be applied with any useful circuit laws and theorems to calculate these parameters.
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the inverting input of the op-amp. The voltage at the node at the intersection between the 200 Ω, 500 Ω, and 600 Ω resistors will be referred to as \(V_X\text{.}\) All resistor units will be expressed in kΩ.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{V_X - 4~\textrm{V}}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{4~\textrm{V} - V_{OUT}}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 1.67V_X - 6.67~\textrm{V} \amp= 4~\textrm{V} - V_{OUT}\\ 1.67V_X + V_{OUT} \amp= 10.67~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node at the intersection between the 200 Ω, 500 Ω, and 600 Ω resistors.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{5~\textrm{V}-V_X}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{V_X - 4~\textrm{V}}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{V_X - V_{OUT}}{0.5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 25~\textrm{V} - 5V_X \amp= 1.67V_X - 6.67~\textrm{V} + 2V_X - 2V_{OUT}\\ 8.67V_X - 2V_{OUT} \amp= 31.67~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(V_X\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(V_{OUT}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1.67 \amp 1 \amp 10.67\\ 8.67 \amp -2 \amp 31.67\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 4.42\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 3.31\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \(V_{OUT}\) is therefore equal to 3.31 V.
    🔗
    Deactivate \(V_2\) to calculate the output voltage contribution from \(V_1\) to calculate \(G_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the inverting input of the op-amp.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{V_X - 4~\textrm{V}}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{4~\textrm{V} - V_{OUT,1}}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 1.67V_X - 6.67~\textrm{V} \amp= 4~\textrm{V} - V_{OUT,1}\\ 1.67V_X + V_{OUT,1} \amp= 10.67~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node at the intersection between the 200 Ω, 500 Ω, and 600 Ω resistors.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{-V_X}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{V_X - 4~\textrm{V}}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{V_X - V_{OUT,1}}{0.5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ - 5V_X \amp= 1.67V_X - 6.67~\textrm{V} + 2V_X - 2V_{OUT,1}\\ 8.67V_X - 2V_{OUT,1} \amp= 6.67~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(V_X\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(V_{OUT,1}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1.67 \amp 1 \amp 10.67\\ 8.67 \amp -2 \amp 6.67\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 2.33\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 6.77\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \(V_{OUT,1}\) is therefore equal to 6.77 V. The source gain from source \(V_1\) can now be calculated.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} G_1 \amp= \frac{6.77~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{V}}\\ \amp= 1.69 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Deactivate \(V_1\) to calculate the output voltage contribution from \(V_2\) to calculate \(G_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the inverting input of the op-amp.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{V_X}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{-V_{OUT,2}}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 1.67V_X \amp= - V_{OUT,2}\\ 1.67V_X + V_{OUT,2} \amp= 0 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node at the intersection between the 200 Ω, 500 Ω, and 600 Ω resistors.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{5~\textrm{V}-V_X}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= \frac{V_X}{0.6~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{V_X - V_{OUT,2}}{0.5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ 25~\textrm{V} - 5V_X \amp= 1.67V_X + 2V_X - 2V_{OUT,2}\\ 8.67V_X - 2V_{OUT,2} \amp= 25~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
