    Section 7.6 Constant-Input Non-Homogeneous Second-Order Circuits

    A non-homogeneous second-order circuit means that \(y(t)\) in the second-order differential equation (described by (7.1.2)) will not be equal to zero.
    🔗
    To solve a non-homogeneous second-order equation, first find the general solution, which is the process used when solving the characteristic equation to calculate the solution to a homogeneous second-order equation. Then, find the particular solution (\(f_P(t)\)). These two solutions are summed together to derive the overall circuit response. Deriving the particular solution is the only new aspect to solving a constant-input non-homogeneous second-order circuit.
    🔗
    The solutions presented below are universally true for all homogeneous and constant-input non-homogeneous second-order circuits. Homogeneous circuits are simply a special case where \(v_P(t) = 0\) or \(i_P(t) = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The value of the particular solution for constant-input non-homogeneous circuits are given in (7.6.1) and (7.6.2).
    🔗
    \begin{align} v_P(t) \amp= v(\infty)\tag{7.6.1}\\ i_P(t) \amp= i(\infty)\tag{7.6.2} \end{align}
    🔗

    Subsection 7.6.1 The Overdamped Solution

    The solution to a constant-input non-homogeneous overdamped circuit is described in (7.6.3), with coefficients \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) defined in (7.6.4) and (7.6.5), respectively. Note that this equation is defined with voltage terms. If calculating current, all \(v(t)\) terms can be replaced by \(i(t)\) terms.
    🔗
    \begin{align} v(t) \amp= \left(v_P(t) + A_1\textrm{e}^{s_1t} + A_2\textrm{e}^{s_2t}\right) u(t) + v(0^-)~u(-t)\tag{7.6.3}\\ A_1 \amp= \frac{s_2(v(0^+)-v(\infty))-v'(0^+)}{s_2-s_1}\tag{7.6.4}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{v'(0^+)-s_1(v(0^+)-v(\infty))}{s_2-s_1}\tag{7.6.5} \end{align}
    🔗
    Coefficients \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) will have a unit corresponding to the quantity being described by the equation. (Volts for voltage, amps or milliamps for current.) These two coefficients are derived by solving (7.6.3) at \(t=0^+\) and by solving the first derivative of (7.6.3) at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) This derivation is shown in Derivation 7.6.1.1.
    🔗

    Derivation.
    Start by solving (7.6.3) when \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0^+) \amp= v_P(0^+) + A_1\textrm{e}^{s_1*0} + A_2\textrm{e}^{s_2*0}\\ v(0^+) \amp= v(\infty) + A_1 + A_2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Calculate the first derivative of (7.6.3) and solve when \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{d}{dt}v(t) \amp= \frac{d}{dt}\left(v_P(0^+) + A_1\textrm{e}^{s_1t} + A_2\textrm{e}^{s_2t}\right)\\ v'(t) \amp= s_1A_1\textrm{e}^{s_1t} + s_2A_2\textrm{e}^{s_2t}\\ v'(0^+) \amp= s_1A_1\textrm{e}^{s_1*0} + s_2A_2\textrm{e}^{s_2*0}\\ v'(0^+) \amp= s_1A_1 + s_2A_2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the equations for \(v(0^+)\) and \(v'(0^+)\) to solve for \(A_1\) and \(A_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A_1 \amp= v(0) - v(\infty) - A_2\\ v'(0^+) \amp= (v(0) - v(\infty) - A_2)s_1 + A_2s_2\\ \amp= v(0^+)s_1 - v(\infty)s_1 - A_2s_1 + A_2s_2\\ A_2 (s_2-s_1) \amp= v'(0^+) - v(0^+)s_1 + v(\infty)s_2\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{v'(0^+) - v(0^+)s_1 + v(\infty)s_2}{s_2-s_1}\\ A_1 \amp= v(0) - v(\infty) - \frac{v'(0^+) - v(0^+)s_1 + v(\infty)s_2}{s_2-s_1}\\ A_1(s_2-s_1) \amp= v(0^+)(s_2-s_1) - v(\infty)(s_2-s_1) - v'(0^+)\\ \amp+ s_1v(0^+) - s_1v(\infty)\\ \amp= v(0^+)s_2 - v(0^+)s_1 + v(0^+)s_1 - v(\infty)s_2\\ \amp+ v(\infty)s_1 - v(\infty)s_1 - v'(0^+)\\ \amp= v(0^+)s_2 - v(\infty)s_2 - v'(0^+)\\ A_1 \amp= \frac{v(0^+)s_2 - v(\infty)s_2 - v'(0^+)}{s_2-s_1} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 7.6.2 The Underdamped Solution

    The solution to a constant-input non-homogeneous underdamped circuit is described in (7.6.6), with coefficients \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) defined in (7.6.7) and (7.6.8), respectively. Note that this equation is defined with voltage terms. If calculating current, all \(v(t)\) terms can be replaced by \(i(t)\) terms.
    🔗
    \begin{align} v(t) \amp= \left(v_P(t) + \textrm{e}^{-\alpha t} \left[B_1\cos{\beta t} + B_2\sin{\beta t}\right]\right)u(t) + v(0^-)~u(-t)\tag{7.6.6}\\ B_1 \amp= v(0^+)-v(\infty)\tag{7.6.7}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{v'(0^+)+\alpha(v(0^+)-v(\infty))}{\beta}\tag{7.6.8} \end{align}
    🔗
    Coefficients \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) will have a unit corresponding to the quantity being described by the equation. (Volts for voltage, amps or milliamps for current.) These two coefficients are derived by solving (7.6.6) at \(t=0^+\) and by solving the first derivative of (7.6.6) at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) This derivation is shown in Derivation 7.6.2.1.
    🔗

    Derivation.
    Start by solving (7.6.6) when \(t=0^+\text{.}\) This conveniently derives coefficient \(B_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0^+) \amp= v_P(0^+) + \textrm{e}^{-\alpha*0} \left[ B_1\cos{(\beta *0)} + B_2\sin{(\beta *0)} \right]\\ v(0^+) \amp= v(\infty) + B_1\\ B_1 \amp= v(0^+) - v(\infty) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Calculate the first derivative of (7.5.4) and solve when \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{d}{dt}v(t) \amp= \frac{d}{dt}\left(v_P(t) + \textrm{e}^{-\alpha t} \left[ B_1\cos{\beta t} + B_2\sin{\beta t} \right]\right)\\ v'(t) \amp= \textrm{e}^{-\alpha t}\left[(\cos{\beta t})(B_2\beta - \alpha B_1) - (\sin{\beta t})(\alpha B_2 + \beta B_1)\right]\\ v'(0^+) \amp= \textrm{e}^{-\alpha *0}\left[(\cos{(\beta *0)})(B_2\beta - \alpha B_1) - (\sin{(\beta *0)})(\alpha B_2 + \beta B_1)\right]\\ v'(0^+) \amp= B_2\beta - \alpha B_1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the equations for \(v(0^+)\) and \(v'(0^+)\) to solve for \(B_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v'(0^+) \amp= B_2\beta - \alpha B_1\\ v'(0^+) - \alpha B_1 \amp= B_2\beta\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{v'(0^+) + \alpha B_1}{\beta}\\ \amp= \frac{v'(0^+) + \alpha (v(0^+) - v(\infty))}{\beta} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 7.6.3 The Critically Damped Solution

    The solution to a constant-input non-homogeneous critically damped circuit is described in (7.6.9), with coefficients \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) defined in (7.6.10) and (7.6.11), respectively. Note that this equation is defined with voltage terms. If calculating current, all \(v(t)\) terms can be replaced by \(i(t)\) terms.
    🔗
    \begin{align} v(t) \amp= \left( v_P(t) + A_1\textrm{e}^{-\alpha t} + A_2t\textrm{e}^{-\alpha t}\right) u(t) + v(0^-)~u(-t)\tag{7.6.9}\\ A_1 \amp= v(0^+)-v(\infty)\tag{7.6.10}\\ A_2 \amp= v'(0^+) + \alpha(v(0^+)-v(\infty))\tag{7.6.11} \end{align}
    🔗
    Coefficient \(A_1\) has a unit corresponding to the quantity being described by the equation. (Volts for voltage, amps or milliamps for current.) Coefficient \(A_2\) has a unit corresponding to the first derivative of the quantity being described by the equation (volts per second for voltage, or amps per second or milliamps per second for current). These two coefficients are derived by solving (7.6.9) at \(t=0^+\) and by solving the first derivative of (7.6.9) at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) This derivation is shown in Derivation 7.6.3.1.
    🔗

    Derivation.
    Start by solving (7.6.9) when \(t=0^+\text{.}\) This conveniently derives coefficient \(A_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0^+) \amp= v_P(0^+) + A_1\textrm{e}^{-\alpha *0} + A_2t\textrm{e}^{-\alpha *0}\\ v(0^+) \amp= v(\infty) + A_1\\ A_1 \amp= v(0^+) - v(\infty) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Calculate the first derivative of (7.6.9) and solve when \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{d}{dt}v(t) \amp= \frac{d}{dt}\left(v_P(t) + A_1\textrm{e}^{-\alpha t} + A_2t\textrm{e}^{-\alpha t}\right)\\ v'(t) \amp= -\alpha A_1\textrm{e}^{-\alpha t} + \textrm{e}^{-\alpha t}(A_2 - A_2\alpha t)\\ v'(0^+) \amp= -\alpha A_1\textrm{e}^{-\alpha *0} + \textrm{e}^{-\alpha *0}(A_2 - A_2\alpha *0)\\ v'(0^+) \amp= -\alpha A_1 + A_2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the equations for \(v(0^+)\) and \(v'(0^+)\) to solve for \(A_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v'(0^+) \amp= -\alpha A_1 + A_2\\ v'(0^+) + \alpha A_1 \amp= A_2\\ A_2 \amp= v'(0^+) + \alpha (v(0^+) - v(\infty)) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 7.6.4 Examples

    Example 7.6.1 Constant-input non-homogeneous general second-order circuit.

    Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) for the circuit below. The switch is in position b for a very long time before moving to position a at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are: \(V_S = 20~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(R_S = 10~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 1~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 10~\Omega\text{,}\) \(L = 20~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) and \(C = 100~\textrm{nF}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The initial parameter values are all equal to zero, as no source is connected to the circuit in the initial conditions. However, it’s possible that \(v_X(t)\) may not be continuous with time. Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) The inductor can be replaced by a 0 A current source and the capacitor can be replaced by a 0 V voltage source.
    🔗
    The 0 A source acts as an open, and the 0 V source acts as a short. Re-draw the circuit.
    🔗
    The value of \(v_X(0^+)\) is equal to zero.
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition to calculate the particular solution, \(v(\infty)\text{.}\) The inductor can be replaced by a short and the capacitor can be replaced by an open.
    🔗
    Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= 20~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{1000~\Omega}{1010~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 19.802~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to derive a differential equation.
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node connecting \(L\text{,}\) \(R_1\text{,}\) and \(R_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} i(t) = \frac{v_X(t)}{R_1} + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\tag{7.6.12} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Perform KVL around the left-hand loop of the circuit.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} V_S = R_Si(t) + L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + v_X(t)\tag{7.6.13} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.6.12) into (7.6.13) to remove \(i(t)\) terms.
    🔗
    \begin{align} V_S \amp= R_S\left(\frac{v_X(t)}{R_1} + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\right) + L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v_X(t)}{R_1} + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\right) + v_X(t)\tag{7.6.14}\\ \amp= \frac{R_S}{R_1}v_X(t) + R_SC\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{L}{R_1}\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} + LC\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + v_X(t)\tag{7.6.15} \end{align}
    🔗
    Perform KVL around the right-hand loop of the circuit. The current through \(R_2\) and the capacitor is defined by (5.3.1).
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_X(t) = R_2C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\tag{7.6.16} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.6.16) into (7.6.15) to remove \(v_X(t)\) terms. Then, reduce to standard form.
    🔗
