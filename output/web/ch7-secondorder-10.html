    Series and Parallel RLC Circuits

    1.

    Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.1. The switch has been closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = -50~\textrm{V/s}~t~\textrm{e}^{-200t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Calculate the initial conditions of the circuit. The inductor can be replaced by a short, and the capacitor can be replaced by an open.
    🔗
    Because the initial voltage drop is measured in parallel with the inductor (short), it will be zero. Use Ohm’s law to calculate the initial current through the inductor.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i_L(0) \amp= \frac{10~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 2.5~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This is a homogeneous series RLC circuit.
    🔗
    Use (7.3.8) to calculate the initial first derivative of the voltage. Note that the current is negative due to the fact that it opposes the direction of the current through the capacitor, which is defined as flowing from high to low potential.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v'(0) \amp= \frac{-0.0025~\textrm{A}}{50~\times10^{-6}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= -50~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.3.5) to calculate the damping parameter.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{200~\Omega}{2(0.5~\textrm{H})}\\ \amp= 200~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.3.6) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(0.5~\textrm{H})(50\times10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= \frac{1}{0.005}\\ \amp= 200~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is critically damped. Use (7.5.8) and (7.5.9) to calculate the coefficients.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A_1 \amp= 0~\textrm{V}\\ A_2 \amp= -50~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug these values into (7.5.7) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = -50~\textrm{V/s}~t \textrm{e}^{-200t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗

    2.

    Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.4. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[20~\textrm{V} + \textrm{e}^{-320000t}\left(6~\textrm{V}~\cos(240000)+3.83~\textrm{V}~\sin(240000t)\right)\right]~u(t) + 26~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Draw the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate the initial current through the inductor.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i_L(0) \amp= \frac{28~\textrm{V} - 20~\textrm{V}}{480~\Omega+160~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{8~\textrm{V}}{640~\Omega}\\ \amp= 12.5~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law over the 160 Ω resistor to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\) (Note that you can also calculate this by using Ohm’s law over the 480 Ω resistor. Try it out if you want extra practice.)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{28~\textrm{V} - v(0)}{0.16~\textrm{k}\Omega} \amp= i_L(0)\\ 28~\textrm{V} - v(0) \amp= (12.5~\textrm{mA})(0.16~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 2~\textrm{V}\\ v(0) \amp= 28~\textrm{V} - 2~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 26~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Simplify the circuit by combining the two parallel resistors.
    🔗
    This is a non-homogeneous series RLC circuit. We will need to derive the final steady-state value of the voltage. Draw the circuit in its final steady-state configuration. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    The value of \(v(\infty)\) is 20 V.
    🔗
    Draw the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Use (7.3.5) to calculate the damping parameter.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{320~\Omega}{2(500\times10^{-6}~\textrm{H})}\\ \amp= 320000~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.3.6) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(500\times10^{-6}~\textrm{H})(12.5\times10^{-9}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= 400000~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \beta \amp= \sqrt{400000^2-320000^2}\\ \amp= 240000~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.3.8) to calculate the initial first derivative of the voltage drop. Note that the current is negative because the current through the inductor flows in the opposite direction as current through the capacitor (which is defined from high to low potential).
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v'(0) \amp= \frac{-0.0125~\textrm{A}}{12.5\times10^{-9}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= -1000000~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.6.7) and (7.6.8) to calculate the coefficients of the underdamped equation.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 26~\textrm{V} - 20~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 6~\textrm{V}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{-1000000 + 320000 [26-20]}{240000}\\ \amp= 3.83~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug these values into (7.6.6) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[20~\textrm{V} + \textrm{e}^{-320000t}\left(6~\textrm{V}~\cos(240000)+3.83~\textrm{V}~\sin(240000t)\right)\right]~u(t) + 26~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    3.

    Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.9. The inductor has a parasitic resistance of 10 Ω, which is not explicitly shown in the circuit diagram. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \left(\textrm{e}^{-27.8t}\left[26~\textrm{V}~\cos{127t} + 5.7~\textrm{V}~\sin{127t}\right]\right)~u(t) + 26~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Calculate the initial conditions of the circuit. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by its parasitic resistance.
    🔗
    The initial voltage is equal to 28 V. There is no current through the inductor in this initial steady-state configuration.
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This is a homogeneous series RLC circuit.
    🔗
    By looking at (7.3.8), we can see that the initial condition for the first derivative of voltage will be equal to zero.
    🔗
    Use (7.3.5) to calculate the damping parameter. Be sure to include the parasitic resistance of the inductor in the resistance value in this calculation!
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{10~\Omega}{2(0.18~\textrm{H})}\\ \amp= \frac{10~\Omega}{0.36~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= 27.7778~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.3.6) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(0.18~\textrm{H})(330 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= \frac{1}{0.0077}\\ \amp= 129.7498~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \beta = \sqrt{(129.7498~\textrm{rad/s})^2 - (27.7778~\textrm{Np/s})^2}\\ \amp= 126.7415~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.5.5) and (7.5.6) to calculate the coefficients.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 26~\textrm{V}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{0~\textrm{V/s} + (27.7778~\textrm{Np/s})(26~\textrm{V})}{126.7415~\textrm{rad/s}}\\ \amp= 5.6984~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug these values into (7.5.4) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \left(\textrm{e}^{-27.8t}\left[26~\textrm{V}~\cos{127t} + 5.7~\textrm{V}~\sin{127t}\right]\right)~u(t) + 26~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    4.

    Derive an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.12. The switch is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[ 117.42~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-8610t} -17.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-58100t}\right]~u(t) + 100~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Draw the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    The 150 Ω resistor is shorted out of the circuit.
    🔗
    Because the initial voltage drop is measured over the inductor (short), it will be zero. This means that \(i'(0)\) will also be equal to zero. Use Ohm’s law to calculate the initial current.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 100~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This is a homogeneous parallel RLC circuit. Use (7.4.5) to calculate the damping parameter.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{1}{2(150~\Omega)(100\times10^{-9}~\textrm{F})}\\ \amp= \frac{1}{3\times10^{-5}}\\ \amp= 33333.33~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.4.6) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(0.02~\textrm{H})(100\times10^{-9}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= \frac{1}{4.47\times10^{-5}}\\ \amp= 22360.68~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -33333.33 + \sqrt{33333.33^2 - 22360.68^2}\\ \amp= -33333.33 + 24720.66\\ \amp= -8612.67\\ s_2 \amp= -33333.33 - \sqrt{33333.33^2 - 22360.68^2}\\ \amp= -33333.33 - 24720.66\\ \amp= -58056.99 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.5.2) and (7.5.3) to calculate the coefficients of the equation.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{(100)(-58056.99)}{-58056.99-(-8612.67)}\\ \amp= 117.42~\textrm{mA}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{- (100)(-8612.67)}{-58056.99-(-8612.67)}\\ \amp= -17.42~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug the values into (7.5.1) to derive the equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[ 117~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-8610t} -17.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-58100t}\right]~u(t) + 100~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[200~\textrm{mA} + \textrm{e}^{-640t}\left(-200~\textrm{mA}~\cos(480t)-267~\textrm{mA}~\sin(480t)\right)\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.

    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This is a non-homogeneous parallel RLC circuit. While this might not seem obvious, consider how the circuit would look after performing a source transformation on the voltage source. This makes the parallel RLC configuration more clear.
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\) The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= \frac{25~\textrm{V}}{125~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.2~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.4.5) to calculate the damping parameter.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{1}{2(125~\Omega)(6.25\times10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}\\ \amp= 640~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.4.6) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(0.25~\textrm{H})(6.25\times10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= 800~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \beta \amp= \sqrt{800^2-640^2}\\ \amp= 480~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.6.7) and (7.6.8) to calculate the coefficients.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 0~\textrm{A} - 0.2~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -0.2~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -200~\textrm{mA}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{640 [0-0.2]}{480}\\ \amp= -0.267~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -266.67~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug these values into (7.6.6) to derive the equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[200~\textrm{mA} + \textrm{e}^{-640t}\left(-200~\textrm{mA}~\cos(480t)-267~\textrm{mA}~\sin(480t)\right)\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}

    Calculate an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.20. The switch is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[ 50.9~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-37.9} -28.5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-67.8}\right]~u(t) + 22.4~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{1}{\frac{1}{20~\Omega}+\frac{1}{10~\Omega}+\frac{1}{35~\Omega}}\\ \amp= 5.6~\Omega \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i(0) \amp= 40~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{5.6~\Omega}{10~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 22.4~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0) \amp= (0.04~\textrm{A})(5.6~\Omega)\\ \amp= 0.224~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This is equivalent to a parallel RLC circuit with parasitic resistance. Use (7.4.15) to calculate the damping parameter. Base units are used for all quantities to ensure the result has a unit of Np/s.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{1}{2(35)(0.005)} + \frac{10}{2(0.1)}\\ \amp= 2.86 + 50\\ \amp= 52.86~\textrm{Np/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.4.16) to calculate the resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \omega_0 \amp= \sqrt{\frac{1}{(0.1)(0.005)}+\frac{10}{(35)(0.1)(0.005)}}\\ \amp= \sqrt{2000+571.43}\\ \amp= 50.71~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    This circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -52.86 + \sqrt{52.86^2 - 50.71^2}\\ \amp= -52.86 + 14.91\\ \amp= -37.94\\ s_2 \amp= -52.86 - \sqrt{52.86^2 - 50.71^2}\\ \amp= -52.86 - 14.91\\ \amp= -67.77 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.4.19) to calculate the initial condition of the first derivative.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i'(0) \amp= \frac{0.224~\textrm{V}- (0.0224~\textrm{A})(10~\Omega)}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= \frac{0.224~\textrm{V} - 0.224~\textrm{V}}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= 0~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.5.2) and (7.5.3) to calculate the coefficients.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{(0.0224)(-67.77)+0}{-67.77-(-37.94)}\\ \amp= 0.0509~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 50.8923~\textrm{mA}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{0 - (0.0224)(-37.94)}{-67.77-(-37.94)}\\ \amp= -0.0285~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -28.4923~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug into (7.5.1) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[ 50.9~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-37.9} -28.5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-67.8}\right]~u(t) + 22.4~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    7.

    Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.23. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are \(L = 5~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 60~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 40~\Omega\text{,}\) and \(C = 2~\mu\textrm{F}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \textrm{e}^{-4900t}\left[1.88~\textrm{V}~\cos(6000 t)+1.53~\textrm{V}~\sin(6000t)\right]~u(t) + 1.88~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i_L(0)\text{.}\) Note the negative sign, which is due to the current direction opposing the source voltage.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i_L(0) \amp= \frac{-5~\textrm{V}}{0.16~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -31.25~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\) This value is also equal to \(v_X(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0) \amp= 5~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{60~\Omega}{100~\Omega+60~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 1.875~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solve for the second order differential equation using symbolic form.
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node connecting \(L\text{,}\) \(R_1\text{,}\) and \(R_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} i_L(t) + \frac{v_X(t)}{R_1} + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} = 0\tag{7.8.1} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Use (6.3.2) to relate the voltage and current through the inductor.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} i_L(t) = \frac{1}{L} \int v_X(t) \,dt\tag{7.8.2} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.8.2) into (7.8.1) to remove \(i_L(t)\) terms from (7.8.1).
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{1}{L} \int v_X(t) \,dt + \frac{v_X(t)}{R_1} + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} = 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Take the first derivative of this equation to remove all integrals.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{1}{L}v_X(t) + \frac{1}{R_1}\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} + C\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} = 0\tag{7.8.3} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Perform KVL around the right loop. Note that the current through \(R_2\) and \(C\) is defined by (5.3.1) because both of those circuit elements are connected in series.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_X(t) = R_2C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\tag{7.8.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.8.4) into (7.8.3), and then reduce to standard form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= \frac{1}{L}\left(R_2C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\right) + \frac{1}{R_1}\frac{d}{dt}\left(R_2C\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\right) + C\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2}\\ \amp= \frac{R_2C}{L}\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{L}v(t) + \frac{R_2C}{R_1}\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{R_1}\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + C\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2}\\ \amp= \left[ \frac{R_2C}{R_1} + C\right]\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left[\frac{R_2C}{L}+\frac{1}{R_1}\right]\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{L}v(t)\\ \amp= \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left[\frac{R_1R_2C+L}{LC(R_1+R_2)}\right]\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \left[\frac{R_1}{LC(R_1+R_2)}\right]v(t) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug in component values.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = \frac{d^2}{dt^2}v(t) + 9800\frac{d}{dt}v(t) + 60000000v(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Calculate the damping parameter and resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= 4900~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= 7745.97~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \beta \amp= \sqrt{7745.97^2-4900^2}\\ \amp= 5999.17~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    (7.8.1) can be used to calculate the initial first derivative of the voltage.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= i_L(0^+) + \frac{1}{R_1}v_X(0^+) + Cv'(0^+)\\ Cv'(0^+) \amp= -i_L(0^+) - \frac{1}{R_1}v_X(0^+)\\ v'(0^+) \amp= \frac{-i_L(0^+) - \frac{1}{R_1}v_X(0^+)}{C} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Before plugging in component values, we must first verify that \(v_X(t)\) is continuous at \(t=0\text{.}\) This is because the voltage is not in parallel with a capacitor, so we cannot take for granted that the value will be 1.875 V at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) with the capacitor replaced by a 1.875 V voltage source and the inductor replaced by a 31.25 mA current source.
    🔗
    Use KCL at the node connecting the current source, \(R_1\text{,}\) and \(R_2\) to calculate \(v_X(0^+)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= -31.25~\textrm{mA} + \frac{v_X(0^+)}{0.06~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{v_X(0^+) - 1.875~\textrm{V}}{0.04~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -31.25~\textrm{mA} + 16.67~\textrm{k}\Omega^{-1}v_X(0^+) + 25v_X(0^+) - 46.875~\textrm{mA}\\ 78.125~\textrm{mA} \amp= 41.67~\textrm{k}\Omega^{-1}v_X(0^+)\\ v_X(0^+) \amp= 1.875~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Now we can calculate the initial condition of the first derivative of the voltage.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \amp= \frac{0.03125~\textrm{A} - \frac{1}{60~\Omega}1.875~\textrm{V}}{2 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= \frac{0.03125~\textrm{A} - 0.03125~\textrm{A}}{2 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= 0~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.5.5) and (7.5.6) to calculate the coefficients of the underdamped equation.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 1.875~\textrm{V}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{4900(1.875)}{5999.17}\\ \amp= 1.531~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug into (7.5.4) to derive the equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = \textrm{e}^{-4900t}\left[1.88~\textrm{V}~\cos(6000 t)+1.53~\textrm{V}~\sin(6000t)\right]~u(t) + 1.88~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    8.

    Derive a second-order differential equation in standard form in terms of \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.27. The switch is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} 0 = \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left[\frac{R_2}{L_1} + \frac{R_2}{L_2} + \frac{R_3}{L_2} + \frac{R_1}{L_1}\right]\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left[\frac{R_1R_2}{L_1L_2} + \frac{R_1R_3}{L_1L_2} + \frac{R_2R_3}{L_1L_2}\right]i(t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node connecting \(L_1\text{,}\) \(R_2\text{,}\) and \(L_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i_X(t) + \frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} + i(t) = 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solve for \(i_X(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} i_X(t) = -\frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} - i(t)\tag{7.8.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Perform KVL around the left loop of the circuit.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_X(t) = L_1 \frac{di_X(t)}{dt} + R_1i_X(t)\tag{7.8.6} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.8.5) into (7.8.6) to remove all \(i_X(t)\) terms from (7.8.6).
    🔗
    \begin{align} v_X(t) \amp= L_1\frac{d}{dt}\left[-\frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} - i(t)\right] + R_1\left[-\frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} - i(t)\right]\tag{7.8.7}\\ 0 \amp= v_X(t) + L_1\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{L_1}{R_2}\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} + R_1i(t) + \frac{R_1}{R_2}v_X(t)\tag{7.8.8} \end{align}
    🔗
    Perform KVL around the right loop of the circuit.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_X(t) = L_2 \frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_3i(t)\tag{7.8.9} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.8.9) into (7.8.8) to remove all \(v_X(t)\) terms from (7.8.8). Then, reduce to standard form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= L_2\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_3i(t) + L_1\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{L_1}{R_2}\frac{d}{dt}\left[L_2\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_3i(t)\right] + R_1i(t) + \frac{R_1}{R_2}\left[L_2\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_3i(t)\right]\\ \amp= L_2\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_3i(t) + L_1\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{L_1L_2}{R_2}\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \frac{R_3L_1}{R_2}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R_1i(t) + \frac{R_1L_2}{R_2}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{R_3R_1}{R_2}i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{L_1L_2}{R_2}\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left[L_1 + L_2 + \frac{L_1R_3}{R_2} + \frac{R_1L_2}{R_2}\right]\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left[R_1 + \frac{R_1R_3}{R_2} + R_3\right]i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left[\frac{R_2}{L_1} + \frac{R_2}{L_2} + \frac{R_3}{L_2} + \frac{R_1}{L_1}\right]\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left[\frac{R_1R_2}{L_1L_2} + \frac{R_1R_3}{L_1L_2} + \frac{R_2R_3}{L_1L_2}\right]i(t) \end{align*}

    9.

    Derive an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.29. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are \(R_D = 20~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R = 10~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C = 400~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(L = 100~\textrm{mH}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \textrm{e}^{-112.5 t} \left[ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~\cos{158 t} + 47.6~\textrm{mA}~\sin{158 t} \right]u(t) + 66.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{4~\textrm{V}}{50~\Omega + 10~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{4~\textrm{V}}{60~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0667~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 66.6667~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v_C(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v_C(0) \amp= 4~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{10~\Omega}{10~\Omega+50~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 0.6667~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Use KCL at the node connecting \(R_D\text{,}\) \(C\text{,}\) and \(L\text{.}\) Use Ohm’s law to derive the current through resistor \(R_D\text{,}\) and (5.3.1) to derive the current through the capacitor.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{v_C(t)}{R_D} + C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} + i(t) = 0\tag{7.8.10} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Use KVL around the right-hand loop of the circuit. Use (6.3.1) to derive the voltage dropped over the inductor.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_C(t) = L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\tag{7.8.11} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Plug (7.8.11) into (7.8.10) to obtain a differential equation in terms of \(i(t)\text{.}\) Then, reduce to standard form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 0 \amp= \frac{1}{R_D}\left(L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\right) + C\frac{d}{dt}\left(L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\right) + i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{L}{R_D}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{R}{R_D}i(t) + LC\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + RC\frac{di(t)}{dt} + i(t)\\ \amp= LC\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{L}{R_D} + RC\right)\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R}{R_D}+1\right)i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{1}{R_DC}+\frac{R}{L}\right)\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R}{LCR_D}+\frac{1}{LC}\right)i(t) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug in parameter values using base units.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + 225\frac{di(t)}{dt} + 37500 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Calculate the values of the damping parameter and resonant frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \alpha \amp= 112.5~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= 193.6492~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \beta \amp= \sqrt{(193.6492~\textrm{rad/s})^2 - (112.5~\textrm{Np/s})^2}\\ \amp= 157.6190~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.8.11) to calculate the initial condition of the first derivative of the current.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v_C(0^+) \amp= Li'(0^+) + Ri(0^+)\\ Li'(0^+) \amp= Ri(0^+) - v_C(0^+)\\ i(0^+) \amp= \frac{Ri(0^+) - v_C(0^+)}{L}\\ \amp= \frac{(10~\Omega)(0.0667~\textrm{A}) - 0.6667~\textrm{V}}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= \frac{0.6667~\textrm{V} - 0.6667~\textrm{V}}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= 0~\textrm{A/s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (7.5.5) and (7.5.6) to calculate the coefficients.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 0.0667~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 66.6667~\textrm{mA}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{0~\textrm{A/s} + (112.5~\textrm{Np/s})(0.0667~\textrm{A})}{157.6190~\textrm{rad/s}}\\ \amp= 0.0476~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 47.5831~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Plug the values into (7.5.4) to obtain an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \textrm{e}^{-112.5 t} \left[ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~\cos{158 t} + 47.6~\textrm{mA}~\sin{158 t} \right]u(t) + 66.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}

    10.

    Derive an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.32. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are \(R_D = 180~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R = 380~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C = 12~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(L = 22~\textrm{mH}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} i(t) = \left(-0.04~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-216t} + 3.04~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-5530t}\right)~u(t) + 3~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state configuration. The inductor can be replaced by a short. The capacitor can be replaced by an open.
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\) This value is continuous with time.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{8.1~\textrm{V}}{2.7~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 3~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
    🔗
    The value of \(v(0^-)\) will be equal to zero, as it’s measured over a short. This is also equal to the voltage dropped over the capacitor (\(v_C(0)\)), which is continuous with time.
    🔗
    The voltage \(v(t)\) may not be continuous with time. Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) Replace the inductor with a 3 mA current source. Replace the capacitor with a 0 V voltage source.
    🔗
    The 0 V source can be replaced by a short to simplify the circuit.
    🔗
    Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= R_D//R\\ \amp= 123~\Omega \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(v(0^+)\text{.}\) Note the negative sign, due to the fact that the current source opposes the polarity of \(v(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} v(0^+) \amp= (-0.003~\textrm{A})(123~\Omega)\\ \amp= -0.3695~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to derive the differential equation.
    🔗
    Perform KCL at the node connecting \(R_D\text{,}\) \(L\text{,}\) and \(R\text{.}\) The current through the branch containing \(R\) and \(C\) is denoted as \(i_C(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{v(t)}{R_D} + i(t) + i_C(t) = 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solve this equation for \(i_C(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} i_C(t) = -\frac{v(t)}{R_D} - i(t)\tag{7.8.12} \end{equation}
    🔗
    The branch containing the resistor and capacitor has a voltage drop of \(v(t)\text{.}\) Relate \(v(t)\) to the voltage drop over both circuit elements using KVL.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} v(t) = Ri_C(t) + \frac{1}{C} \int i_C(t) \,dt \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Take the derivative of this equation to eliminate any integrals.
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{dv(t)}{dt} = R\frac{di_C(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{C}i_C(t)\tag{7.8.13} \end{equation}
    🔗

    Derive the differential equation in terms of \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.70. Assume that the op-amp is sufficiently sourced such that the output will never saturate.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} -\frac{1}{R_1C_2}\frac{dv_{in}}{dt} = \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{1}{C_1R_F}+\frac{1}{C_2R_F}\right)\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{1}{C_1C_2R_1R_F}+\frac{1}{C_1C_2R_2R_F}\right)v(t) \end{equation*}

    🔗
    Solution.
    Perform KCL at the node connecting \(R_1\text{,}\) \(R_2\text{,}\) \(C_1\text{,}\) and \(C_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{v_{in}(t) - v_X(t)}{R_1} = \frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} + C_2 \frac{d}{dt}\left(v_X(t) - v(t)\right) + C_1\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt}\tag{7.8.45} \end{equation}

    Perform KCL at the inverting input of the op-amp. Then, solve for \(v_X(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
