    Phasor Arithmetic

    1.

    Convert \(-200-j100\) to polar form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} 224 \angle -153^\circ \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use (8.1.2) to calculate the magnitude.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} r \amp= \sqrt{(-200)^2+(-100)^2}\\ \amp= 224 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Use (8.1.3) to calculate the angle.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \theta \amp= \atantwo(-100,-200)\\ \amp= -153^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Convert \(5\angle -120^\circ\) to Cartesian form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} -2.5 - j4.33 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use sine and cosine to calculate the real and imaginary parts.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 5\cos(-120^\circ) + j5\sin(-120^\circ) = -2.5 - j4.33 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Calculate \(\vec{Z} = (\vec{Z_1})(\vec{Z_2}) + \vec{Z_3}\) given the phasor values below. Express the result in both Cartesian and polar forms.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 10+j20\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= -30+j50\\ \vec{Z_3} \amp= -15-j40 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= -1315 - j140\\ \amp= 1320 \angle -174^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, multiply \(\vec{Z_1}\) and \(\vec{Z_2}\text{.}\) If performing the multiplication by hand, convert to polar form first.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 22.36 \angle 63.43^\circ\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= 58.31 \angle 120.96^\circ\\ \vec{Z_1}\vec{Z_2} \amp= (22.36)(58.31)~\textrm{e}^{j(63.43^\circ+120.96^\circ)}\\ \amp= 1303.84~\textrm{e}^{j184.3987}\\ \amp= 1303.84 \angle 184.3987\\ \amp= -1300 -j100 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Second, add the result to \(\vec{Z_3}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= (-1300 -j100) + (-15-j40)\\ \amp= (-1300 + (-15)) + j(-100 + (-40))\\ \amp= -1315 - j140 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Convert the result to polar form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= \sqrt{(-1315)^2 + (-140)^2} \angle \atantwo(-140,-1315)\\ \amp= 1320 \angle -174^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Calculate \(\vec{Z} = (\vec{Z_1} - \vec{Z_2})(\vec{Z_3})\) given the phasor values below. Express the result in both Cartesian and polar forms.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 10\angle60^\circ\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= 4\angle-140^\circ\\ \vec{Z_3} \amp= 20\angle20^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= 277 \angle 74.3^\circ\\ \amp= 74.7 + j266 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, subtract \(\vec{Z_2}\) from \(\vec{Z_1}\text{.}\) If performing the subtraction by hand, convert to Cartesian form first.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 5+j8.66\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= -3.06-j2.57\\ \vec{Z_1} - \vec{Z_2} \amp= (5 - (-3.06)) + j(8.66 - (-2.57))\\ \amp= 8.06+j11.23\\ \amp= 13.8266 \angle 54.3216^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    Second, multiply the result with \(\vec{Z_3}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= (13.8266 \angle 54.3216^\circ)(20\angle20^\circ)\\ \amp= (13.8266)(20)~\textrm{e}^{j(54.3216^\circ + 20^\circ)}\\ \amp= 277~\textrm{e}^{j74.3216^\circ}\\ \amp= 277 \angle 74.3^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    Convert the result to Cartesian form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= 276.53\cos{74.32^\circ} +j276.53\sin{74.32^\circ}\\ \amp= 74.7 + j266 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Calculate \(\vec{Z} = (\vec{Z_1})(\vec{Z_2}) + \frac{\vec{Z_3}}{\vec{Z_4}}\) given the phasor values below. Express the result in both Cartesian and polar forms.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 10+j14\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= 60\angle-140^\circ\\ \vec{Z_3} \amp= 38\angle20^\circ\\ \vec{Z_4} \amp= -5-j18 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= 79.1 - j1030\\ \amp= 1030 \angle -85.6^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Convert \(\vec{Z_1}\) to polar form, then multiply \(\vec{Z_1}\) and \(\vec{Z_2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= 17.2047 \angle 54.4623^\circ\\ \vec{Z_1}\vec{Z_2} \amp= (17.2047 \angle 54.4623^\circ)(60\angle-140^\circ)\\ \amp= (17.2047)(60)~\textrm{e}^{j(54.4623^\circ + (-140^\circ))}\\ \amp= 1032.3~\textrm{e}^{-j85.5377^\circ}\\ \amp= 1032.3 \angle -85.5377^\circ\\ \amp= 80.315 - j1029.1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Convert \(\vec{Z_4}\) to polar form, then divide \(\vec{Z_3}\) by \(\vec{Z_4}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_4} \amp= 18.6815 \angle -105.5241^\circ\\ \frac{\vec{Z_3}}{\vec{Z_4}} \amp= \frac{38\angle20^\circ}{18.6815 \angle -105.5241^\circ}\\ \amp= \frac{38}{18.6815}~\textrm{e}^{j(20^\circ - (-105.5241^\circ))}\\ \amp= 2.0341~\textrm{e}^{j125.5241^\circ}\\ \amp= 2.0341 \angle 125.5241^\circ\\ \amp= -1.1819 + j1.6555 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Add the results of the last two operations.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= (80.315 - j1029.1) + (-1.1819 + j1.6555)\\ \amp= (80.315 + (-1.1819)) + j(-1029.1 + 1.6555)\\ \amp= 79.133 - j1027.5 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Convert the result to polar form.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z} \amp= \sqrt{79.133^2 + (-1027.5)^2} \angle \atantwo(-1027.5,79.133)\\ \amp= 1030.5 \angle -85.5960^\circ \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    10.

    Calculate the equivalent impedance between nodes a and b of the circuit shown in Figure 8.4.8. The frequency of operation is 6 kHz.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} 0.0586 - j0.528~\Omega \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use (8.2.5) to calculate the impedance of the inductor.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} j(2\pi6000)(33\times10^{-6}) = j1.24~\Omega \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of each capacitor.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{1}{j(2\pi 6000)(50\times10^{-6})} \amp= -j0.53~\Omega\\ \frac{1}{j(2\pi 6000)(68\times10^{-6})} \amp= -j0.39~\Omega \end{align*}
    🔗
    The 5 Ω resistor is connected in series with the j1.24 Ω impedance.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (5~\Omega) + (j1.24~\Omega) = 5 + j1.24~\Omega \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The 6 Ω resistor is connected in series with the −j0.39 Ω impedance.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (6~\Omega) + (-j0.39~\Omega) = 6 - j0.39~\Omega \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The 5+j1.24 Ω impedance is connected in parallel with the 6−j0.39 Ω impedance.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{(5 + j1.24~\Omega)(6 - j0.39~\Omega)}{(5 + j1.24~\Omega)+(6 - j0.39~\Omega)} = 2.7935 + j0.2844 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The 2 Ω resistor is connected in series with the 2.8+j0.28 Ω impedance.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 2~\Omega + (2.7935 + j0.2844) = 4.7935 + j0.2844 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The −j0.53 Ω impedance is connected in parallel with the 4.8+j0.28 Ω impedance.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \vec{Z_{EQ}} \amp= \frac{(-j0.53~\Omega)(4.7935 + j0.2844)}{(-j0.53~\Omega)+(4.7935 + j0.2844)}\\ \amp= 0.0586 - j0.528~\Omega \end{align*}
