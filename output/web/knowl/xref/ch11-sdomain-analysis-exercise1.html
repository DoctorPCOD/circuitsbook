Exercise 11.

Use \(s\)-domain analysis to calculate \(V(s)\) and \(v(t)\) of the circuit shown in Figure 11.4.1. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The component values are: \(V_{S1} = 3~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(V_{S2} = 2~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 50~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 100~\Omega\text{,}\) and \(C = 250~\mu\textrm{F}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} V(s) \amp= \frac{-6s - 160}{s(s+40)}\\ v(t) \amp= \left[-4~\textrm{V} - 2~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-40t}\right]~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Calculate the initial conditions of the circuit. At \(t \gt 0\) the switch is in position a and the capacitor can be treated as an open.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= -V_{S1}\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)\\ \amp= -3~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{100~\Omega}{50~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= -6~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Transform the circuit into the \(s\)-domain.
🔗
Use KCL at the inverting node of the op-amp to derive an equation in terms of \(V(s)\text{.}\) Then, solve for \(V(s)\) and normalize.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{V_{S2}}{sR_1} \amp= \frac{-V(s)}{R_2} + \frac{-V(s) + \frac{v(0)}{s}}{\frac{1}{sC}}\\ \amp= \frac{-V(s)}{R_2} - sCV(s) + Cv(0)\\ -\frac{V_{S2}}{sR_1} + Cv(0) \amp= \frac{V(s)}{R_2} + sCV(s)\\ -\frac{V_{S2}}{sCR_1} + v(0) \amp= \frac{V(s)}{CR_2} + sV(s)\\ V(s) \amp= \frac{sv(0) - \frac{V_{S2}}{CR_1}}{s(s+\frac{1}{CR_2})} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug in component values using base units.
🔗
\begin{equation*} V(s) = \frac{-6s - 160}{s(s+40)} \end{equation*}
🔗
The partial fraction expansion will take the form shown below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} V(s) = \frac{K_1}{s} + \frac{K_2}{(s+40)} \end{equation*}
🔗
To calculate coefficient \(K_1\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s)\) and solve at \(s=-0\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{(-6s - 160)(s)}{s(s+40)}\Big|_{s=0} \amp= \frac{K_1(s)}{s}\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_2(s)}{(s+40)}\Big|_{s=0}\\ \frac{-6s - 160}{s+40}\Big|_{s=0} \amp= K_1\Big|_{s=0} + \frac{K_2(s)}{(s+40)}\Big|_{s=0}\\ \frac{-6(0) - 160}{0+40} \amp= K_1 + \frac{K_2(0)}{(0+40)}\\ \frac{-160}{40} \amp= K_1\\ K_1 \amp= -4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
To calculate coefficient \(K_2\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+40)\) and solve at \(s=-40\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{(-6s - 160)(s+40)}{s(s+40)}\Big|_{s=-40} \amp= \frac{K_1(s+40)}{s}\Big|_{s=-40} + \frac{K_2(s+40)}{(s+40)}\Big|_{s=-40}\\ \frac{-6s - 160}{s}\Big|_{s=-40} \amp= \frac{K_1(s+40)}{s}\Big|_{s=-40} + K_2\Big|_{s=-40}\\ \frac{-6(-40) - 160}{(-40)} \amp= \frac{K_1((-40)+40)}{(-40)} + K_2\\ \frac{80}{-40} \amp= \frac{K_1(0)}{-40} + K_2\\ K_2 \amp= -2~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
The expanded form of \(V(s)\) is given below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} V(s) = \frac{-4}{s} + \frac{-2}{(s+40)} \end{equation*}
🔗
Consult Table 11.1.1 to derive the time-domain function.
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[-4~\textrm{V} - 2~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-40t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
