Exercise 14.

Use \(s\)-domain analysis to calculate \(I(s)\) and \(i(t)\) of the circuit shown in Figure 11.4.9. The switch is in position b for a very long time before moving to position a at a time of zero seconds. The component values are: \(V_{S1} = 40~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(\alpha = 4~\textrm{rad/s}\text{,}\) \(V_{S2} = 10~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(R = 50~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C = 10~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(L = 400~\textrm{mH}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} I(s) \amp= \frac{-25s^2 -100s -400}{(s+4)^2\left(s^2 + 125s + 2.5\textrm{E}5\right)}\\ i(t) \amp= \biggl[-1.6~\textrm{mA/s}~t~\textrm{e}^{-4t} + 0.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-4t}\\ \amp+ \textrm{e}^{-62.5t}\left(-0.4~\textrm{mA}~\cos(496t) - 50.4~\textrm{mA}~\sin(496t)\right)\biggr]~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Calculate the initial conditions of the circuit. The inductor acts like a short and the capacitor acts like an open. For this reason, \(i(0)\) is zero and \(v(0)\) is equal to \(V_{S2}\text{.}\) Transform the circuit into the \(s\) domain.
🔗
Perform KVL around the loop to obtain an equation in terms of \(I(s)\text{.}\) Then, solve for \(I(s)\) and normalize.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{V_{S1}}{(s+\alpha)^2} - \frac{V_{S2}}{s} \amp= RI(s) + sLI(s) + \frac{I(s)}{sC}\\ \frac{sCV_{S1}}{(s+\alpha)^2} - CV_{S2} \amp= sCRI(s) + s^2LCI(s) + I(s)\\ \frac{sV_{S1}}{L(s+\alpha)^2} - \frac{V_{S2}}{L} \amp= s^2I(s) + \frac{R}{L}sI(s) + \frac{1}{LC}I(s)\\ \frac{sV_{S1}-V_{S2}(s+\alpha)^2}{L(s+\alpha)^2} \amp= s^2I(s) + \frac{R}{L}sI(s) + \frac{1}{LC}I(s)\\ \frac{sV_{S1}-V_{S2}s^2 - s2\alpha V_{S2} - V_{S2}\alpha^2}{L(s+\alpha)^2} \amp= s^2I(s) + \frac{R}{L}sI(s) + \frac{1}{LC}I(s) \end{align*}
🔗
Solve for \(I(s)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I(s) \amp= \frac{\left[\frac{-V_{S2}}{L}\right]s^2 + \left[\frac{V_{S1}-2\alpha V_{S2}}{L}\right]s + \left[\frac{-V_{S2}\alpha^2}{L}\right]}{(s+\alpha)^2\left(s^2+\left[\frac{R}{L}\right]s + \left[\frac{1}{LC}\right]\right)}\\ \amp= \frac{-25s^2 -100s -400}{(s+4)^2\left(s^2 + 125s + 2.5\textrm{E}5\right)} \end{align*}
🔗
Factor the denominator.
🔗
\begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{-25s^2 -100s -400}{(s+4)^2(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)} \end{equation*}
🔗
The partial fraction expansion will take the form shown below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{K_1}{(s+4)^2} + \frac{K_2}{(s+4)} + \frac{K_3}{(s+62.5+j496)} + \frac{K_4}{(s+62.5-j496)} \end{equation*}
🔗
To calculate coefficient \(K_1\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+4)^2\) and solve at \(s=-4\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \amp\frac{(-25s^2 -100s -400)(s+4)^2}{(s+4)^2(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+4)^2}{(s+4)^2}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+4)^2}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5+j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp\frac{-25s^2 -100s -400}{(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp= K_1\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ K_2(s+4)\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5+j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp\frac{-25(-4)^2 -100(-4) -400}{((-4)+62.5+j496)((-4)+62.5-j496)}\\ \amp= K_1\\ \amp+ K_2(0)\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(0)}{58.5+j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(0)}{58.5-j496}\\ K_1 \amp= \frac{-400}{249516}\\ \amp= -0.0016~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -1.6031~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
To calculate coefficient \(K_2\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+4)^2\text{,}\) differentiate with respect to \(s\text{,}\) then solve at \(s=-4\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \amp\frac{d}{ds}\frac{(-25s^2 -100s -400)(s+4)^2}{(s+4)^2(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp= \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_1(s+4)^2}{(s+4)^2}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_2(s+4)^2}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_3(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5+j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_4(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp\frac{d}{ds}\frac{-25s^2 -100s -400}{(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp= \frac{d}{ds}K_1\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}K_2(s+4)\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_3(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5+j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{d}{ds}\frac{K_4(s+4)^2}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp\frac{-3025(s^2+4131.97s+8247.93)}{(s^2+125s+2.5\textrm{E}5)^2}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp= 0\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ K_2\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+4)(s+121+j992)}{(s+62.5+j496)^2}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+4)(s+121-j992)}{(s+62.5-j496)^2}\Big|_{s=-4}\\ \amp\frac{-3025((-4)^2+4131.97(-4)+8247.93)}{((-4)^2+125(-4)+2.5\textrm{E}5)^2}\\ \amp= K_2\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(0)}{(58.5+j496)^2}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(0)}{(58.5-j496)^2}\\ K_2 \amp= \frac{2.4998\textrm{E}7}{6.2258\textrm{E}10}\\ \amp= 4.0153\textrm{E}-4~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 0.4015~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
To calculate \(K_3\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+62.5+j496)\) and solve at \(s=-62.5-j496\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \amp\frac{(-25s^2 -100s -400)(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+4)^2(s+62.5+j496)(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+4)^2}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+62.5+j496)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp\frac{(-25s^2 -100s -400)}{(s+4)^2(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+4)^2}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+4)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ K_3\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+62.5+j496)}{(s+62.5-j496)}\Big|_{s=-62.5-j496}\\ \amp\frac{(-25(-62.5-j496)^2 -100(-62.5-j496) -400)}{((-62.5-j496)+4)^2((-62.5-j496)+62.5-j496)}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(0)}{(-58.5-j496)^2}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(0)}{-58.5-j496}\\ \amp+ K_3 + \frac{K_4(0)}{-j992}\\ K_3 \amp= \frac{6.0605\textrm{E}6 - j1.5006\textrm{E}6}{5.7586\textrm{E}7 + j2.4077\textrm{E}8} \\ \amp= -0.0002 - j0.0252~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -0.2008 - j25.2197~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
\(K_4\) is the complex conjugate of \(K_3\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} K_4 = -0.2008 + j25.2197~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
The expanded form of \(I(s)\) is given below. (The unit of each numerator is mA.)
🔗
\begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{-1.603}{(s+4)^2} + \frac{0.4}{(s+4)} + \frac{-0.201 -j25.220}{(s+62.5+j496)} + \frac{-0.201 +j25.220}{(s+62.5-j496)} \end{equation*}
🔗
Consult Table 11.1.1 to derive the time-domain function.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \biggl[-1.6~\textrm{mA/s}~t~\textrm{e}^{-4t} + 0.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-4t}\\ \amp+ \textrm{e}^{-62.5t}\left(-0.4~\textrm{mA}~\cos(496t) - 50.4~\textrm{mA}~\sin(496t)\right)\biggr]~u(t) \end{align*}
