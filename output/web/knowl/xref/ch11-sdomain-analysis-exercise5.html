Exercise 15.

Use \(s\)-domain analysis to calculate \(I(s)\) and \(i(t)\) of the circuit shown in Figure 11.4.11. The switch closes at a time of zero seconds. The component values are: \(I_m = 500~\textrm{mA}\text{,}\) \(\omega = 60~\textrm{rad/s}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 200~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 2~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_3 = 1~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C = 20~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(L = 50~\textrm{mH}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} I(s) \amp= \frac{9.95s^2 + 497512.44s}{(s^2+3600)\left(s^2 + 308.66s + 1004975.12\right)}\\ i(t) \amp= \biggl[500~\textrm{mA}~\cos(60t) + 8.59~\textrm{mA}~\sin(60t)\\ \amp+ \textrm{e}^{-154t}\left(-500~\textrm{mA}~\cos(991t) - 67.9~\textrm{mA}~\sin(991t)\right)\biggr]~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The initial current through the inductor and initial voltage drop over the capacitor are both zero, as no source is connected before the switch closes. Transform the circuit into the \(s\) domain.
🔗
Perform KCL and KVL to obtain an equation in terms of \(I(s)\text{.}\) Then, solve for \(I(s)\) and normalize.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{I_ms}{s^2+\omega^2} \amp= \frac{V(s)}{R_1} + I(s) + \frac{V(s)}{R_3+\frac{1}{sC}}\\ V(s) \amp= sLI(s) + R_2I(s)\\ \frac{I_ms}{s^2+\omega^2} \amp= \frac{sLI(s)}{R_1} + \frac{R_2I(s)}{R_1} + I(s) + \frac{sLI(s) + R_2I(s)}{R_3 + \frac{1}{sC}}\\ \left(\frac{I_ms}{s^2+\omega^2}\right)\left(R_3 + \frac{1}{sC}\right) \amp= \frac{sLI(s)}{R_1}\left(R_3 + \frac{1}{sC}\right) + \frac{R_2I(s)}{R_1}\left(R_3 + \frac{1}{sC}\right)\\ \amp+ I(s)\left(R_3 + \frac{1}{sC}\right) + sLI(s) + R_2I(s)\\ \frac{I_mR_3s}{s^2+\omega^2} + \frac{I_m}{C(s^2+\omega^2)} \amp= \frac{sLR_3I(s)}{R_1} + \frac{L}{R_1C}I(s)\\ \amp+ \frac{R_2R_3I(s)}{R_1} + \frac{R_2}{sCR_1}I(s)\\ \amp+ R_3I(s) + \frac{1}{sC}I(s)\\ \amp+ sLI(s) + R_2I(s)\\ \frac{CI_mR_3s^2 + I_ms}{C(s^2+\omega^2)} \amp= \left[\frac{LR_3}{R_1} + L\right]s^2I(s)\\ \amp+ \left[\frac{L}{R_1C} + \frac{R_2R_3}{R_1} + R_3 + R_2\right]sI(s)\\ \amp+ \left[\frac{R_2}{CR_1} + \frac{1}{C}\right]I(s)\\ \frac{CI_mR_1R_3s^2 + R_1I_ms}{LC(R_1+R_3)(s^2+\omega^2)} \amp= s^2I(s)\\ \amp+ \left[\frac{1}{C(R_1+R_3)} + \frac{R_2R_3+R_1R_3+R_1R_2}{L(R_1+R_3)}\right]sI(s)\\ \amp+ \left[\frac{R_1+R_2}{LC(R_1+R_3)}\right]I(s) \end{align*}
🔗
\begin{align*} I(s) \amp= \frac{\left[\frac{CR_1R_3I_m}{LC(R_1+R_3)}\right]s^2 + \left[\frac{R_1I_m}{LC(R_1+R_3)}\right]s}{(s^2+\omega^2)\left(s^2+\left[\frac{1}{C(R_1+R_3)} + \frac{R_2R_3 + R_1R_3 + R_1R_2}{L(R_1+R_3)}\right]s + \left[\frac{R_1+R_2}{LC(R_1+R_3)}\right]\right)}\\ \amp= \frac{9.95s^2 + 497512.44s}{(s^2+3600)\left(s^2 + 308.66s + 1004975.12\right)} \end{align*}
🔗
Factor the denominator.
🔗
\begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{9.95s^2 + 497512.44s}{(s+j60)(s-j60)(s+154+j991)(s+154-j991)} \end{equation*}
🔗
The partial fraction expansion will take the form shown below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} I(s) = \frac{K_1}{(s+j60)} + \frac{K_2}{(s-j60)} + \frac{K_3}{(s+154+j991)} + \frac{K_4}{(s+154-j991)} \end{equation*}
🔗
To calculate \(K_1\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+j60)\) and solve at \(s=-j60\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \amp\frac{(9.95s^2 + 498000s)(s+j60)}{(s+j60)(s-j60)(s+154+j991)(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+j60)}{(s+j60)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+j60)}{(s-j60)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+j60)}{(s+154+j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+j60)}{(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp\frac{9.95s^2 + 498000s}{(s-j60)(s+154+j991)(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp= K_1\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+j60)}{(s-j60)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+j60)}{(s+154+j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+j60)}{(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-j60}\\ \amp\frac{9.95(-j60)^2 + 498000(-j60)}{((-j60)-j60)((-j60)+154+j991)((-j60)+154-j991)}\\ \amp= K_1\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(0)}{-j120}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(0)}{154+j931}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(0)}{154-j1051}\\ K_1 \amp= \frac{-3.5821\textrm{E}4 - j2.9851\textrm{E}7}{-2.2223\textrm{E}6 - j1.2017\textrm{E}8}\\ \amp= 0.2483 + j0.0043~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 248.34 + j4.2946~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
\(K_2\) is the complex conjugate of \(K_1\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} K_2 = 248.34 - j4.2946~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
To calculate \(K_3\text{,}\) multiply both sides of the equation by \((s+154+j991)\) and solve at \(s=-154-j991\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \amp\frac{(9.95s^2 + 498000s)(s+154+j991)}{(s+j60)(s-j60)(s+154+j991)(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+154+j991)}{(s+j60)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+154+j991)}{(s-j60)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_3(s+154+j991)}{(s+154+j991)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+154+j991)}{(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp\frac{9.95s^2 + 498000s}{(s+j60)(s-j60)(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(s+154+j991)}{(s+j60)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(s+154+j991)}{(s-j60)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ K_3\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_4(s+154+j991)}{(s+154-j991)}\Big|_{s=-154-j991}\\ \amp\frac{9.95(-154-j991)^2 + 498000(-154-j991)}{(-154-j931)(-154-j1051)(-j1982)}\\ \amp= \frac{K_1(0)}{-154-j931}\\ \amp+ \frac{K_2(0)}{-154-j1051}\\ \amp+ K_3 + \frac{K_4(0)}{-j1982}\\ K_3 \amp= \frac{-8.6306\textrm{E}7 - j4.8976\textrm{E}8}{6.0568\textrm{E}8 + j1.8894\textrm{E}9}\\ \amp= -0.2483 - j0.0339~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= -248.34 - j33.929~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
\(K_4\) is the complex conjugate of \(K_3\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} K_4 = -248.34 + j33.929~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
The expanded form of \(I(s)\) is given below. (The unit of each numerator is mA.)
🔗
\begin{align*} I(s) \amp= \frac{248.34 + j4.2946}{(s+j60)} + \frac{248.34 - j4.2946}{(s-j60)}\\ \amp+ \frac{-248.34 - j33.929}{(s+154+j991)} + \frac{-248.34 + j33.929}{(s+154-j991)} \end{align*}
🔗
Consult Table 11.1.1 to derive the time-domain function.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \biggl[500~\textrm{mA}~\cos(60t) + 8.59~\textrm{mA}~\sin(60t)\\ \amp+ \textrm{e}^{-154t}\left(-500~\textrm{mA}~\cos(991t) - 67.9~\textrm{mA}~\sin(991t)\right)\biggr]~u(t) \end{align*}
