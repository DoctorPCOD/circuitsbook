Exercise 20.

Calculate \(V_X\) in the circuit shown in Figure 2.10.45.
🔗
Answer.
120 V
🔗
🔗
Solution.
As drawn, the circuit contains zero perfect nodes and zero perfect loops. To remedy this, the branch current through the 100 kΩ resistor will be defined. The re-drawn circuit is shown below.
🔗
The circuit now has one perfect node and one perfect loop. There are two unknowns, so two equations will be derived. All units are in V, mA, and kΩ and will be omitted from the KCL and KVL equations to make them easier to read.
🔗
Node a is a perfect node. Using KCL at this node we obtain one equation.
🔗
\begin{equation*} 0.2I_X - I_X - I_1 = 0 \end{equation*}
🔗
The right mesh is a perfect loop. Using KVL around this loop (starting in the lower left and moving clockwise), we obtain the second equation.
🔗
\begin{equation*} -48I_X + 100I_1 + 320 = 0 \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(I_X\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(I_1\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} -0.8 \amp -1 \amp 0\\ -48 \amp 100 \amp -320 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 2.5\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp -2\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
Read the first row to determine that \(I_X = 2.5~\textrm{mA}\text{.}\) Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(V_X\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_X \amp= (2.5~\textrm{mA})(48~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 120~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
This circuit can also be solved in a similar manner to Exercise 2.10.18. However, it may not be as intuitive. The equations below use units of V, kΩ, and mA and will not be explicitly shown. We know that
🔗
\begin{equation*} 0.2I_X = I_X + I_1\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
In addition, we know that
🔗
\begin{align*} I_X \amp=\frac{V_X}{48}\\ I_1 \amp= \frac{V_X - 320}{100}\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗
Use these three equations to solve for \(V_X\) directly.
🔗
\begin{align*} 0.2\left(\frac{V_X}{48}\right) \amp= \frac{V_X}{48} + \frac{V_X - 320}{100}\\ \frac{V_X}{240} \amp= \frac{V_X}{48} + \frac{V_X - 320}{100}\\ \frac{5V_X}{1200} \amp= \frac{25V_X}{1200} + \frac{12V_X - 3840}{1200}\\ 5V_X \amp= 25V_X + 12V_X - 3840\\ -32V_X \amp= -3840\\ V_X = 120~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
