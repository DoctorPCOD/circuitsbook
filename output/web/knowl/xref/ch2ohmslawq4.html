Exercise 9.

Calculate the amount of power supplied by the source in the circuit shown in Figure 2.10.29.
🔗
Hint.
Use conservation of power.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
234 W
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The circuit can be reduced to an equivalent circuit containing one 5 A source and one resistor. Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the source to calculate this resistance value. (The 50 Ω and 100 Ω resistors are in series. This series resistance is in parallel with the 10 Ω resistor.)
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= (50~\Omega + 100~\Omega) // (10~\Omega)\\ \amp= 150~\Omega // 10~\Omega\\ \amp = 9.375~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Conservation of power states that the power supplied by the source must be equal to the power consumed by this equivalent resistance. Use (2.4.5) to calculate the power consumed by \(R_{EQ}\) (which is equal to the power supplied by the source).
🔗
\begin{align*} P \amp= I^2R\\ \amp= (5~\textrm{A})^2(9.375~\Omega)\\ \amp= (25~\textrm{A}^2)(9.375~\Omega)\\ \amp= 234~\textrm{W} \end{align*}
🔗
Alternatively, a combination of Ohm’s law and power calculations (or current divider and power calculations) can be used to calculate the power consumed by each individual resistor (10 Ω, 50 Ω, and 100 Ω) and those power values can be summed to calculate the total power consumption, which is equal to the power supplied by the source. This process will not be shown in this book but can be used for extra practice. This calculation will also yield 234 W.
