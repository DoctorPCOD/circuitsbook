Exercise 1.

Use superposition to calculate \(V_X\) in the circuit shown in Figure 3.8.1.
🔗
Answer.
12.7 V
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Draw the circuit with only the voltage source activated. Deactivate the current source by replacing it with an open.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(V_{X1}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{X1} \amp= 13~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{560~\Omega}{390~\Omega+560~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 7.66~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit with only the current source activated. Deactivate the voltage source by replacing it with a short.
🔗
Combine the resistors in parallel and use Ohm’s law to calculate \(V_{X2}\text{.}\) (Because the two resistors are in parallel, \(V_{X2}\) drops over both resistors.)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{X2} \amp= (560~\Omega//390~\Omega)(0.022~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= (229.89~\Omega)(0.022~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= 5.06~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Add the two values to calculate the voltage \(V_X\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_X \amp= V_{X1} + V_{X2}\\ \amp= 7.66~\textrm{V} + 5.06~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 12.7~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
