Exercise 15.

Derive the Norton equivalent circuit between nodes a and b in the circuit shown in Figure 3.8.76.
🔗
Answer.
🔗
Solution.
First, reduce the circuit as much as possible. The two resistors in the left mesh of the circuit are in series and can be combined. The two voltage sources are also in series and can be combined. The reduced circuit is shown below.
🔗
The circuit contains a dependent source. Therefore, the open-circuit voltage will be calculated along with the Norton equivalent current to calculate the Norton equivalent resistance. First, use Ohm’s law to calculate \(I_X\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_X \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}-10V_X}{250~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.08~\textrm{A} - (0.04~\textrm{A/V})V_X \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(V_X\text{,}\) which is the open-circuit voltage.
🔗
\begin{align*} V_X \amp= (100~\Omega)(0.5I_X)\\ \amp= (100~\Omega)(0.5)(0.08~\textrm{A} - (0.04~\textrm{A/V})V_X)\\ \amp= 4~\textrm{V} - 2V_X\\ 3V_X \amp= 4~\textrm{V}\\ V_X \amp= 1.33~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Short the nodes between a and b and re-draw the circuit. \(V_X\) is now equal to zero. This is because \(V_X\) is measured over the 100 Ω resistor, which is now shorted out, and by definition has a voltage drop of zero. This causes the VCVS in the left mesh to contribute no voltage to the circuit. The reduced circuit is shown below.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(I_X\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_X \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}}{250~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.08~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Use KCL to calculate \(I_N\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_N \amp= 0.5I_X\\ \amp= 0.5(0.08~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= 0.04~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the Norton equivalent resistance.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_N \amp= \frac{1.33~\textrm{V}}{0.04~\textrm{A}}\\ \amp= 33.3~\Omega \end{align*}
