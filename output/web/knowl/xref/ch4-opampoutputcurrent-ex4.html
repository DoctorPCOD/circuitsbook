Exercise 14.

The LM118 op-amp chip can sink up to 65 mA of current. Calculate the smallest load resistor that can be used in the circuit shown in Figure 4.8.19. Then, calculate the power consumed by that resistor.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} R_{LOAD} \amp= 1.24~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ P_{LOAD} \amp= 1.04~\textrm{W} \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The op-amp circuit is an inverting summing op-amp. Apply (4.5.5) to calculate \(V_{OUT}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= (-1~\textrm{k}\Omega)\left(\frac{3~\textrm{V}}{0.5~\textrm{k}\Omega}+\frac{6~\textrm{V}}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (-1~\textrm{k}\Omega)(6~\textrm{mA}+30~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= (-1~\textrm{k}\Omega)(36~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= -36~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the current through the 1 kΩ feedback resistor that flows into the output node of the op-amp.
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{F} \amp= \frac{0~\textrm{V} - (-36~\textrm{V})}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 36~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
The op-amp can only sink 65 mA of current. Calculate the current that can flow into the output node of the op-amp through the load resistor.
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{LOAD} \amp= 65~\textrm{mA} - 36~\textrm{mA}\\ \amp= 29~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the minimum load resistance that can be used in the circuit so as not to exceed 29 mA.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{LOAD,MIN} \amp= \frac{0~\textrm{V} - (-36~\textrm{V})}{29~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= \frac{36~\textrm{V}}{29~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= 1.24~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (2.4.5) to calculate the power consumed by the resistor.
🔗
\begin{align*} P_{LOAD} \amp= (29~\textrm{mA})^2(1.24~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= (841~\textrm{mA}^2)(1.24~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 1,040~\textrm{mW}\\ \amp= 1.04~\textrm{W} \end{align*}
