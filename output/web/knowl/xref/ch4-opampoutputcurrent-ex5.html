Exercise 15.

The LM741 op-amp chip can source up to 25 mA of current. Calculate the smallest load resistor that can be used in the circuit shown in Figure 4.9.20. Then, calculate the power consumed by that resistor.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} R_{LOAD} \amp= 1.14~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ P_{LOAD} \amp= 502~\textrm{mW} \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The op-amp circuit is a non-inverting op-amp. Apply (4.6.3) and (4.6.1) to calculate \(V_{OUT}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= (4~\textrm{V})\left(1+\frac{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (4~\textrm{V})(6)\\ \amp= 24~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the current through the 5 kΩ feedback resistor that flows out of the output node of the op-amp.
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{F} \amp= \frac{24~\textrm{V} - 4~\textrm{V}}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 4~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
The op-amp can only source 25 mA of current. Calculate the current that can flow out of the output node of the op-amp through the load resistor.
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{LOAD} \amp= 25~\textrm{mA} - 4~\textrm{mA}\\ \amp= 21~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the minimum load resistance that can be used in the circuit so as not to exceed 21 mA.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{LOAD,MIN} \amp= \frac{24~\textrm{V}-0~\textrm{V}}{21~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= \frac{24~\textrm{V}}{21~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= 1.14~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (2.4.5) to calculate the power consumed by the resistor.
🔗
\begin{align*} P_{LOAD} \amp= (21~\textrm{mA})^2(1.14~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= (441~\textrm{mA}^2)(1.14~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 502~\textrm{mW} \end{align*}
