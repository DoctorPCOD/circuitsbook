Exercise 8.

Calculate the value of \(R_{LOAD}\) that should be used in Figure 4.9.9 to maximize the power transferred to that load resistor. Then, calculate the power consumed by \(R_{LOAD}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} R_{LOAD} \amp= 4~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ P_{MAX} \amp= 39.1~\textrm{mW} \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Calculate \(V_{OC}\) and \(I_{SC}\) to calculate \(R_{TH}\text{,}\) which is the resistance used to obtain the maximum power transferred to the load. The circuit is a non-inverting op-amp. Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(V_P\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_P \amp= 9~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}{8~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (4.6.3) to calculate the gain of the op-amp circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} G \amp= 1+\frac{20~\textrm{k}\Omega}{5~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 5 \end{align*}
🔗
\(V_{OC}\) is equal to the output voltage of the circuit. (Because the output is an open-circuit, no current flows through the 4 kΩ resistor, and it does not contribute to the circuit.) Use (4.6.1) to calculate this voltage.
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{OUT} \amp= GV_{P}\\ \amp= (5)(5~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= 25~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
The short-circuit current is equal to the current between the output of the op-amp and ground when the load resistor is shorted. The output voltage of the op-amp will be 5 V and the other end of the 4 kΩ resistor will be grounded. This leads to a short-circuit current of
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{SC} \amp= \frac{5~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 6.25~\textrm{mA}. \end{align*}
🔗
The Thévenin equivalent resistance can now be calculated. This is equal to the load resistance that should be used to maximize the power transferred to the load.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{TH} \amp= \frac{25~\textrm{V}}{6.25~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= 4~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (3.7.2) to calculate the maximum power transferred to the load.
🔗
\begin{align*} P_{MAX} \amp= \frac{(25~\textrm{V})^2}{4(4~\textrm{k}\Omega)}\\ \amp= \frac{625~\textrm{V}^2}{16~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 39.1~\textrm{mW} \end{align*}
