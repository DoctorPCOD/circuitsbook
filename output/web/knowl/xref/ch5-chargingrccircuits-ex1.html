Exercise 17.

Calculate an expression for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.45. The switch is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= 4.44~\textrm{V}\left(1 - \textrm{e}^{-90t}\right)~u(t)\\ i(t) \amp= 2~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-90t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
In the initial steady-state, the capacitor will be fully discharged and have an initial voltage of zero. Draw the circuit in the final steady-state. The capacitor can be replaced as an open.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\) (Refer to Example 2.5.5 for using the voltage divider rule in a two-mesh circuit.)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= 20~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega//(4~\textrm{k}\Omega+4~\textrm{k}\Omega)}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega+2~\textrm{k}\Omega//(4~\textrm{k}\Omega+4~\textrm{k}\Omega)}\right)\left(\frac{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}{8~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 20~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega//8~\textrm{k}\Omega}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega+2~\textrm{k}\Omega//8~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\\ \amp= 20~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{1.6~\textrm{k}\Omega}{3.6~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\\ \amp= 4.44~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit at \(t=0^+\) and deactivate the source to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= (2~\textrm{k}\Omega//2~\textrm{k}\Omega+4~\textrm{k}\Omega)//4~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= (1~\textrm{k}\Omega+4~\textrm{k}\Omega)//4~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{k}\Omega//4~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 2.22~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to calculate the RC time constant.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= RC\\ \amp= (2222~\Omega)\left(5\times10^{-6}~\textrm{F}\right)\\ \amp= 0.011~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug these values into (5.7.12) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 4.44~\textrm{V}\left(1 - \textrm{e}^{-90t}\right)~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (5.7.13) to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 2~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-90t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
