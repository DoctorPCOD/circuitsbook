Exercise 23.

Derive equations for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.81. The switch is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= \left[26.7~\textrm{mV} + 13.3~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-6250000t} \right] u(t)\\ i(t) \amp= \left[6.67~\textrm{mA} + 3.33~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-6250000t} \right] u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The capacitor has an initial voltage of 0 V. This value must be continuous with time (i.e. \(v_C(0^-) = v_C(0^+) = 0~\textrm{V}\)). The resistor will have an initial voltage of 0 V as well (due to not being connected to the source), but this value may not be continuous with time. To calculate \(v(0^+)\text{,}\) analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\) Treat the capacitor as a source providing \(v_C(0)\) volts.
🔗
No voltage drops over the 2 Ω resistor (this is due to KVL; it is in parallel with a 0 V source and therefore must have the same voltage drop). Therefore, all 40 mV provided by the source must be dropped over the 4 Ω resistor.
🔗
Now analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor. (The capacitor will not be treated as a source at this point, as the goal is to calculate equivalent resistance and not voltage.) Deactivate the 40 mV voltage source by converting it to a short.
🔗
The two resistors are in parallel. Calculate the equivalent resistance.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{(2~\Omega)(4~\Omega)}{2~\Omega + 4~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{8~\Omega^2}{6~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.33~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= RC\\ \amp= (1.33~\Omega)(120 \times 10^{-9}~\textrm{F})\\ \amp= 1.6 \times 10^{-7}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\) Treat the capacitor as an open circuit.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= (40~\textrm{mV})\left(\frac{4~\Omega}{2~\Omega + 4~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (40~\textrm{mV})(0.667)\\ \amp= 26.7~\textrm{mV} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.14) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= \left[v(\infty) + \left( v(0^+) - v(\infty) \right) \textrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right] u(t) + v(0^-)u(-t)\\ \amp= \left[26.7~\textrm{mV} + 13.3~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-6250000t} \right] u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Because the voltage and current are measured over a resistor, Ohm’s law will be used to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R}\\ \amp = \frac{\left[26.7~\textrm{mV} + 13.3~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-6250000t} \right] u(t)}{4~\Omega}\\ \amp= \left[6.67~\textrm{mA} + 3.33~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-6250000t} \right] u(t) \end{align*}
