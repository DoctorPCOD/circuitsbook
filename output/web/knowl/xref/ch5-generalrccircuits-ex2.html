Exercise 22.

Calculate an expression for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.71. The switch is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= \left[ 4~\textrm{V} + 10~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1000t}\right]~u(t) + 14~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\\ i(t) \amp= -0.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-1000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Draw the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Convert both sources to current sources. The 18 V source becomes
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{18~\textrm{V}}{10~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 1.8~\textrm{mA}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The 16 V source becomes
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{16~\textrm{V}}{50~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 0.32~\textrm{mA}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the two sets of parallel resistors.
🔗
Convert the 0.32 mA source to a voltage source. The voltage becomes
🔗
\begin{equation*} (0.32~\textrm{mA})(25~\textrm{k}\Omega) = 8~\textrm{V}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the two series resistors.
🔗
Convert the voltage source to a current source. The value of the current source is
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{8~\textrm{V}}{50~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 0.16~\textrm{mA}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the two sources and combine the two parallel resistors.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= (1.96~\textrm{mA})(7.14~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 14~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\) (Refer to Example 2.5.5 for using the voltage divider rule in a two-mesh circuit.)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= 16~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{50~\textrm{k}\Omega//75~\textrm{k}\Omega}{50~\textrm{k}\Omega+50~\textrm{k}\Omega//75~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{50~\textrm{k}\Omega}{75~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 16~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{30~\textrm{k}\Omega}{50~\textrm{k}\Omega+30~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)\\ \amp= 4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit at \(t=0^+\) and deactivate the source to calculate the equivalent resistance seen by the capacitor.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= (50~\textrm{k}\Omega//50~\textrm{k}\Omega+25~\textrm{k}\Omega)//50~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= (25~\textrm{k}\Omega+25~\textrm{k}\Omega)//50~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 50~\textrm{k}\Omega//50~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 25~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to calculate the RC time constant.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= RC\\ \amp= (25000~\Omega)(40\times10^{-9}\textrm{F})\\ \amp= 0.001~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug these values into (5.7.14) to derive \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[ 4~\textrm{V} + 10~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1000t}\right]~u(t) + 14~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (5.7.15) to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = -0.4~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-1000t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
