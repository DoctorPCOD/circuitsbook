Exercise 24.

Derive equations for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.85. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The switch then moves to position c at a time of 15 ms and remains in that position for a long time. Assume that the op-amp is sufficiently sourced such that the output will never saturate.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= -10~\textrm{V} + \left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t) - \left[-4~\textrm{V} - 2.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) + \left[-2~\textrm{V} - 4.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015)\\ i(t) \amp= -0.1~\textrm{mA} + \left[-0.04~\textrm{mA}-0.06~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t) - \left[-0.04~\textrm{mA} - 0.0283~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) + \left[-0.02~\textrm{mA} - 0.0483~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
First, analyze the circuit in its initial condition to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\) The switch is in position a and the capacitor acts as an open circuit. Only the 5 V source is connected in this configuration.
🔗
The circuit is an inverting op-amp. Use (4.5.2) to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= -(5~\textrm{V})\left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= -10~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
This means that the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(-10~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\text{,}\) representing the initial steady-state conditions of the circuit. This creates a \(v(t)\) graph that so far appears as follows.
🔗
Analyze the circuit with the switch in position b to determine how the circuit will act between 0 ms and 15 ms. Only the 2 V source is connected in this configuration.
🔗
Derive a differential equation to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\) To derive this symbolically, the voltage source will be referred to as \(V_S\text{,}\) the 1 kΩ resistor will be referred to as \(R_1\text{,}\) the 2 kΩ resistor will be referred to as \(R_2\text{,}\) the 100 kΩ resistor will be referred to as \(R_3\text{,}\) and the capacitor will be referred to as \(C\text{.}\) Use KCL at the inverting node of the op-amp to derive the differential equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{V_S}{R_1} \amp= \frac{0-v(t)}{R_2} + C \frac{d}{dt}\left(0-v(t)\right)\\ \amp= -\frac{v(t)}{R_2} - C\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ -\frac{V_S}{CR_1} \amp= \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{CR_2}v(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use coefficient \(a_0\) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{1}{a_0}\\ \amp= CR_2\\ \amp= (10 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})(2000~\Omega)\\ \amp= 0.02~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Note that because the coefficient \(a_0\) in the differential equation is only a function of the 2 kΩ resistor, the value of \(\tau\) will not change based on the position of the switch.
🔗
Calculate the value of \(v(\infty_b)\text{,}\) which is the output voltage that would occur if the switch were to remain in position b for a very long time. The capacitor acts as an open.
🔗
The circuit is an inverting op-amp. Use (4.5.2) to calculate \(v(\infty_b)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty_b) \amp= -(2~\textrm{V})\left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= -4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.15) with \(v(0) = -10~\textrm{V}\) and \(v(\infty) = v(\infty_b)\) to derive an equation for how the voltage will act between 0 s and the time the switch moves from position b to position c. This term is equal to \(\left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing both terms derived so far appears below.
🔗
Before analyzing the circuit with the switch in position c, calculate the value of \(v(t)\) at 15 ms, which represents the “initial condition” experienced by the capacitor when that switch closes.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0.015) \amp= \left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-(50)(0.015)}\right]\\ \amp= \left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.75}\right]\\ \amp= \left[-4~\textrm{V}-(6~\textrm{V})(0.472)\right]\\ \amp= \left[-4~\textrm{V}-2.83~\textrm{V}\right]\\ \amp= -6.83~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
At 15 ms, the exponential term describing the switch in position b no longer dictates how the circuit operates. This term must be subtracted out at \(t = 0.015~\textrm{s}\text{.}\) Therefore, the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(-\left[-4~\textrm{V}-2.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing all three terms is shown below.
🔗
The value of \(\tau\) remains unchanged when the switch moves to position c. However, the value of \(v(\infty)\) can be calculated by analyzing the circuit in its final steady-state condition. The capacitor will be treated as an open.
🔗
The circuit is an inverting op-amp. Use (4.5.2) to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= -(1~\textrm{V})\left(\frac{2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= -2~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.15) with \(v(0.015) = -6.83~\textrm{V}\) and \(v(\infty) = -2~\textrm{V}\) to derive an equation for how the voltage will act after 15 ms. This term is equal to \(\left[-2~\textrm{V}-4.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing all terms derived so far appears below.
🔗
The full equation for \(v(t)\) is expressed below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = -10~\textrm{V} + \left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t) - \left[-4~\textrm{V} - 2.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) + \left[-2~\textrm{V} - 4.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) \end{equation*}
🔗
The current \(i(t)\) is measured through a 100 kΩ resistor. Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R}\\ \amp= \frac{-10~\textrm{V} + \left[-4~\textrm{V}-6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t) - \left[-4~\textrm{V} - 2.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) + \left[-2~\textrm{V} - 4.83~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015)}{100~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= -0.1~\textrm{mA} + \left[-0.04~\textrm{mA}-0.06~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50t}\right]~u(t) - \left[-0.04~\textrm{mA} - 0.0283~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) + \left[-0.02~\textrm{mA} - 0.0483~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-50(t-0.015)}\right]~u(t-0.015) \end{align*}
