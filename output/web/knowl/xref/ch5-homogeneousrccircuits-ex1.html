Exercise 11.

Derive equations for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.25. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= 200~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-62.5t}~u(t) + 200~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\\ i(t) \amp= -5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-62.5t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Draw the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be represented as an open. Remove any circuit elements that are disconnected from the capacitor by the switch.
🔗
No current flows through the 10 kΩ resistor, so it does not contribute to \(v(0)\text{.}\) The quantity \(v(0)\) is therefore equal to the voltage dropped over the 20 kΩ resistor, which can be calculated using Ohm’s law.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= (10~\textrm{mA})(20~\textrm{k}\Omega)\\ \amp= 200~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance seen by the capacitor. The circuit is shown in this condition below. Remove any circuit elements that are disconnected from the capacitor by the switch.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= 75~\textrm{k}\Omega//50~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 30~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 40~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (5.7.5) to calculate the RC time constant.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= (40000~\Omega)(400\times10^{-9}~\textrm{F})\\ \amp= 0.016~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug these values into (5.7.4) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 200~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-62.5t}~u(t) + 200~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to derive an equation for the current.
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = -5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-62.5t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
