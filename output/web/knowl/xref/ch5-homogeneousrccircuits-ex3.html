Exercise 13.

Based on the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.31, calculate the value of \(R_X\) needed to cause the capacitor to discharge to \(1/e\) of its initial voltage value at a time of 5 s. Then, derive equations for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) based on that resistor value. The switch is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} R_X \amp= 125~\Omega\\ v(t) \amp= 7.5~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.2t}~u(t) + 7.5~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\\ i(t) \amp= -60~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-0.2t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The time constant \(\tau\) describes the amount of time it takes a discharging capacitor to reach \(1/e\) of its initial value. Therefore, \(\tau = 5~\textrm{s}\text{.}\) This statement is proved below using (5.7.4).
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{v(0)}{e} \amp= v(0)~\textrm{e}^{-\frac{5~\textrm{s}}{\tau}}\\ \frac{1}{e} \amp= \textrm{e}^{-\frac{5~\textrm{s}}{\tau}}\\ \ln{\frac{1}{e}} \amp= \ln{\textrm{e}^{-\frac{5~\textrm{s}}{\tau}}}\\ -1 \amp= -\frac{5~\textrm{s}}{\tau}\\ 1 \amp= \frac{5~\textrm{s}}{\tau}\\ \tau \amp= 5~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Now, use (5.7.5) to calculate \(R_X\) to achieve this time constant value. This is the only resistor seen by the capacitor after the switch moves to the lower position.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= RC\\ 5~\textrm{s} \amp= R_X(40 \times 10^{-3}~\textrm{F})\\ R_X \amp= \frac{5~\textrm{s}}{40 \times 10^{-3}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= 125~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state configuration to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\) The capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= 9~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{125~\Omega}{25~\Omega+125~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 7.5~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug values into (5.7.4) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 7.5~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.2t}~u(t)+7.5~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \frac{-7.5~\textrm{V}}{0.125~\textrm{k}\Omega}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{5}}~u(t)\\ \amp= -60~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-0.2t}~u(t) \end{align*}
