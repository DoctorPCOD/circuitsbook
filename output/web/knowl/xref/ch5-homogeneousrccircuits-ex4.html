Exercise 14.

The capacitor circuit shown in Figure 5.8.33 has a discharging equation of \(v(t) = 3.5~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1000t}~u(t)+3.5~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\text{.}\) Derive the values of \(R_1\) and \(R_2\text{.}\) (To derive the discharging equation, the switch was closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} R_1 \amp= 2000~\Omega\\ R_2 \amp= 1000~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The only resistor involved in discharging the capacitor is \(R_2\text{.}\) Therefore, (5.7.5) can be used to calculate the value of \(R_2\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= RC\\ \frac{1}{1000~\textrm{s}^{-1}} \amp= R_2 (1 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})\\ R_2 \amp= \frac{1}{(1000~\textrm{s}^{-1})(1 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}\\ \amp= 1000~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the initial conditions of the circuit to calculate the value of \(R_1\text{.}\) The circuit diagram for this scenario is shown below.
🔗
We know from the voltage equation and (5.7.4) that the initial voltage \(v(0) = 3.5~\textrm{V}\text{.}\) Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(R_1\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= V_S\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\right)\\ 3.5~\textrm{V} \amp= 10.5~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{1000~\Omega}{R_1+1000~\Omega}\right)\\ \frac{1}{3} \amp= \frac{1000~\Omega}{R_1+1000~\Omega}\\ \frac{1}{3}(R_1+1000~\Omega) \amp= 1000~\Omega\\ R_1+1000~\Omega \amp= 3000~\Omega\\ R_1 \amp= 2000~\Omega \end{align*}
