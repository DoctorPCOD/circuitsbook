Exercise 15.

In the circuit shown in Figure 5.8.35, switch \(S_1\) is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of 0 ms. Switch \(S_2\) is open for a very long time before closing at a time of 1 ms. Derive equations for \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= 30~\textrm{V}~u(-t) + 30~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-667t}~u(t) - 15.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\\ \amp+ 15.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001) \\ i(t) \amp= -1~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-667t}~u(t) + 0.513~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\\ \amp- 0.513~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-1667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
First, analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state condition. Switch \(S_1\) is closed and switch \(S_2\) is open. The circuit diagram is shown below.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0) \amp= 40~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{30~\Omega}{10~\Omega+30~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 30~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
This means that the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(30~\textrm{V}~u(-t)\text{,}\) representing the initial steady-state conditions of the circuit. This creates a \(v(t)\) graph that so far appears as follows.
🔗
Analyze the circuit with both switches open to determine how the circuit will act between 0 ms and 1 ms.
🔗
The equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor is 30 Ω. Use (5.7.5) to calculate the value of \(\tau\) for this interval of time.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= (30~\Omega)(50 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})\\ \amp= 0.0015~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
This means that the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(30~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-667t}~u(t)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing both terms appears below.
🔗
Before analyzing the circuit when \(S_2\) closes, calculate the value of \(v(t)\) at 1 ms, which represents the “initial condition” experienced by the capacitor when that switch closes.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(0.001) \amp= 30~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-(667)(0.001)}\\ \amp= 30~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.667}\\ \amp= 30~\textrm{V}~(0.513)\\ \amp= 15.4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
At 1 ms, the exponential term describing the decay with \(\tau = 0.0015~\textrm{s}\) no longer dictates how the circuit operates. This term must be subtracted out at \(t = 0.001~\textrm{s}\text{.}\) Therefore, the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(-15.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing all three terms is shown below.
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0.001^+~\textrm{s}\) to calculate the new value of \(\tau\) with both switches in their final positions.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance seen by the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{(30~\Omega)(20~\Omega)}{30~\Omega + 20~\Omega}\\ \amp= 12~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
The equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor is 12 Ω. Use (5.7.5) to calculate the value of \(\tau\) for this final time period.
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= (12~\Omega)(50 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})\\ \amp= 0.0006~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
This means that the equation for \(v(t)\) will contain a term of \(15.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\text{.}\) The graph of the voltage containing all four terms appears below.
🔗
Use (5.7.6) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\) The equivalent resistance is 30 Ω for the terms described with a time constant of 0.0015 s, and 12 Ω for the term described with a time constant of 0.0006 s.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= -\frac{30~\textrm{V}}{30~\Omega}~\textrm{e}^{-667t}~u(t) + \frac{15.4~\textrm{V}}{30~\Omega}~\textrm{e}^{-667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\\ \amp- \frac{15.4~\textrm{V}}{12~\Omega}~\textrm{e}^{-1667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\\ \amp= -1~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-667t}~u(t) + 0.513~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001)\\ \amp- 0.513~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-1667(t-0.001)}~u(t-0.001) \end{align*}
