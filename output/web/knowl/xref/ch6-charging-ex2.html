Exercise 17.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.60. The switch has been in position b for a very long time before moving to position a at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 0.0346~\textrm{A}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-102.6t}\right]~u(t)\\ v(t) \amp= 2.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-102.6t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\) The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= \frac{2.7~\textrm{V}}{78~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0346~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Deactivate the voltage source.
🔗
The equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor is 78 Ω. Use (6.5.4) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{0.760~\textrm{H}}{78~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0097~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.6) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 0.0346~\textrm{A}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-102.6t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.7) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (78~\Omega)\left(0.0346~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-102.6t}~u(t)\right)\\ \amp= 2.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-102.6t}~u(t) \end{align*}
