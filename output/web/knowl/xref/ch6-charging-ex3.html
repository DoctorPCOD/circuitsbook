Exercise 18.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.64. The switch has been open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 20~\textrm{mA}~\left[1 - \textrm{e}^{-1667t}\right]~u(t)\\ v(t) \amp= 5~\textrm{V}~\left[1 - \textrm{e}^{-1667t}\right]~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
First, simplify the circuit by combining the two series resistors.
🔗
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use source transformation to convert the voltage source into a current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{10~\textrm{V}}{0.2~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 50~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{1}{\frac{1}{200~\Omega}+\frac{1}{250~\Omega}+\frac{1}{1000~\Omega}}\\ \amp= 100~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
The voltage \(v(\infty)\) is dropped in parallel with all three resistors. Use Ohm’s law with the source current and \(R_{EQ}\) to calculate this value.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= (0.1~\textrm{k}\Omega)(50~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law through the branch with the 250 Ω resistor to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= \frac{5~\textrm{V}}{0.25~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 20~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Deactivate the voltage source.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= (200~\Omega // 1000~\Omega) + 250~\Omega\\ \amp= 166.67~\Omega + 250~\Omega\\ \amp= 416.67~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.4) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{0.25~\textrm{H}}{416.67~\Omega}\\ \amp= 6 \times 10^{-4}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.6) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 20~\textrm{mA}~\left[1 - \textrm{e}^{-1667t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (0.25~\textrm{k}\Omega)(20~\textrm{mA}\left[1 - \textrm{e}^{-1667t}\right]~u(t))\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{V}~\left[1 - \textrm{e}^{-1667t}\right]~u(t) \end{align*}
