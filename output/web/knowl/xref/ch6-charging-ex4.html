Exercise 19.

Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.70. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. Assume the op-amp is sufficiently sourced such that the output will not saturate. The parameter values are \(L = 100~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) \(R_F = 330~\Omega\text{,}\) and \(V_S = 0.8~\textrm{V}\text{.}\) The inductor has a parasitic resistance of 70 Ω.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 4.57~\textrm{V}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-700t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by its parasitic resistance.
🔗
This is a non-inverting op-amp circuit. Use (4.5.3) to calculate the gain of the op-amp.
🔗
\begin{align*} G \amp= 1 + \frac{R_F}{R_P}\\ \amp= 1 + \frac{330~\Omega}{70~\Omega}\\ \amp= 5.714 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (4.1.1) to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= GV_S\\ \amp= (5.714)(0.8~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= 4.57~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\text{.}\) The parasitic resistance will be explicitly drawn in this circuit to make it clear.
🔗
Derive a differential equation in terms of \(v(t)\text{.}\) Use KCL at the inverting input of the op-amp.
🔗
\begin{equation} i_L(t) = \frac{V_S - v(t)}{R_F}\tag{6.6.3} \end{equation}
🔗
Use KVL over the inductor and its parasitic resistance.
🔗
\begin{equation} 0 - V_S = R_Pi_L(t) + L\frac{di_L(t)}{dt}\tag{6.6.4} \end{equation}
🔗
Plug (6.6.3) into (6.6.4), and reduce until the differential equation is in standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} -V_S \amp= R_P\left(\frac{V_S - v(t)}{R_F}\right) + L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{V_S - v(t)}{R_F}\right)\\ \amp= \frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S - \frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) + L\frac{d}{dt}(V_S) - L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v(t)}{R_F}\right)\\ \amp= \frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S - \frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) - L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v(t)}{R_F}\right)\\ -V_S - \frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S \amp= - \frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) - L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v(t)}{R_F}\right)\\ V_S + \frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S \amp= \frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) + L\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v(t)}{R_F}\right)\\ V_S + \frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S \amp= \frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) + \frac{L}{R_F}\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \left(\frac{R_F}{L}\right)V_S + \left(\frac{R_F}{L}\right)\frac{R_P}{R_F}V_S \amp= \left(\frac{R_F}{L}\right)\frac{R_P}{R_F}v(t) + \left(\frac{R_F}{L}\right)\frac{L}{R_F}\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \frac{R_F}{L} V_S + \frac{R_P}{L}V_S \amp= \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{R_P}{L}v(t)\\ \left(\frac{R_F + R_P}{L}\right)V_S \amp= \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{R_P}{L}v(t)\\ 3200 \amp= \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + 700 v(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.6) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 4.57~\textrm{V}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-700t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
