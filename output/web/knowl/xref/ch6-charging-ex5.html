Exercise 20.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.73. The switch has been open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are \(V_S = 9.7~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 8~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 60~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(L = 8.2~\textrm{H}\text{,}\) and \(k_i = 0.22\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 1.48~\textrm{mA}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-0.7209t}\right]~u(t)\\ v(t) \amp= 8.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.7209t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Resistor \(R_2\) is shorted out and can be removed from the circuit.
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting the three branches of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= i_X(\infty) + k_ii_X(\infty)\\ \amp= (1 + k_i)i_X(\infty) \end{align*}
🔗
Use KVL around the outer loop of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} V_S \amp= R_1 i_X(\infty)\\ i_X(\infty) \amp= \frac{V_S}{R_1} \end{align*}
🔗
Use the two equations to solve for \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= (1 + k_i)\left(\frac{V_S}{R_1}\right)\\ \amp= (1+0.22)\left(\frac{9.7~\textrm{V}}{8~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (1.22)(1.2125~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= 1.48~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor. There are two methods that can be used to calculate this value. The first method is to derive the Thévenin equivalent circuit as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Calculate the open-circuit voltage. Define branch current \(i_2(t)\) as the current through resistor \(R_2\text{.}\)
🔗
Use KVL around the left-hand mesh of the circuit to obtain one equation in terms of \(i_X(t)\) and \(i_2(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} R_1i_X(t) + R_2i_2(t) = V_S \end{equation*}
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting the three branches of the circuit to derive a second equation in terms of \(i_X(t)\) and \(i_2(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i_X(t) + k_ii_X(t) = i_2(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations are expressed below with numerical values. The units of V, mA, and  kΩ are used, but are not explicitly shown.
🔗
\begin{align*} 8i_X(t) + 60i_2(t) \amp= 9.7\\ 1.22i_X(t) - i_2(t) \amp= 0 \end{align*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(i_X(t)\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(i_2(t)\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 8 \amp 60 \amp 9.7\\ 1.22 \amp -1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0.1195\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0.1457\\ \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The open-circuit voltage is equal to \(R_2i_2(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{OC} \amp=R_2i_2(t)\\ \amp= (60~\textrm{k}\Omega)(0.1457~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= 8.74~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Short the two terminals of the circuit to calculate the short-circuit current.
🔗
Resistor \(R_2\) is shorted out and can be removed from the circuit.
🔗
Use KVL around the outer loop of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} V_S \amp= R_1i_X(t)\\ i_X(t) \amp= \frac{V_S}{R_1} \end{align*}
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting the three circuit branches.
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{SC} \amp= i_x(t) + k_ii_X(t)\\ \amp= (1+k_i)i_X(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use both equations to solve for \(I_{SC}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{SC} \amp= (1+k_i)\left(\frac{V_S}{R_1}\right)\\ \amp= (1.22)\left(\frac{9.7~\textrm{V}}{8~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (1.22)(1.2125~\textrm{mA})\\ \amp= 1.48~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(R_{TH}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{TH} \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{I_{SC}}\\ \amp= \frac{8.74~\textrm{V}}{1.48~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= 5.91~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
The second method is to derive the differential equation from the circuit.
🔗
Use KVL around the outer loop of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{equation} V_S = R_1i_X(t) + L\frac{di(t)}{dt}\tag{6.6.5} \end{equation}
🔗
Use KCL at the node that connects the four circuit branches. Note that the voltage dropped over resistor \(R_2\) is the same as the voltage dropped over the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align} i_X(t) \amp= \frac{L\frac{di(t)}{dt}}{R_2} - k_ii_X(t) + i(t)\tag{6.6.6}\\ i_X(t) + k_ii_X(t) \amp= \frac{L}{R_2}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + i(t)\tag{6.6.7}\\ i_X(t) (1+k_i) \amp= \frac{L}{R_2}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + i(t)\tag{6.6.8}\\ i_X(t) \amp= \frac{L}{R_2(1+k_i)}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{1+k_i}i(t)\tag{6.6.9} \end{align}
🔗
Plug (6.6.9) into (6.6.5) and reduce until the differential equation is in standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} V_S \amp= R_1\left(\frac{L}{R_2(1+k_i)}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{1+k_i}i(t)\right) + L\frac{di(t)}{dt}\\ \amp= \left(\frac{R_1L}{R_2(1+k_i)}+L\right) \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R_1}{1+k_i}\right)i(t)\\ \amp= \left(\frac{L(R_1+R_2(1+k_i))}{R_2(1+k_i)}\right) \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R_1}{1+k_i}\right)i(t)\\ \frac{V_SR_2(1+k_i)}{L(R_1+R_2(1+k_i))} \amp= \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{R_2R_1}{L(R_1+R_2(1+k_i))}i(t)\\ 1.066 \amp= \frac{di(t)}{dt} + 0.7209i(t) \end{align*}
🔗
This yields the same result as the Thévenin equivalent circuit method, as expected. If we had not already derived the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor, we could use (6.5.4) to solve for \(R\text{.}\)
🔗
Use (6.5.6) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 1.48~\textrm{mA}~\left[1-\textrm{e}^{-0.7209t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.7) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (5.91~\textrm{k}\Omega)(1.48~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-0.7209t}~u(t))\\ \amp= 8.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.7209t}~u(t) \end{align*}
