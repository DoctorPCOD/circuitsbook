Exercise 2.

Calculate the equivalent inductance of the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.4.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} L_{EQ} = 102.2~\mu\textrm{H} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Start by combining the 40 µH and 50µH inductors in parallel.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(50~\mu\textrm{H})(40~\mu\textrm{H})}{50~\mu\textrm{H}+40~\mu\textrm{H}} = 22.22~\mu\textrm{H} \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the 30 µH, 22.2 µH, and 20 µH inductors in series.
🔗
\begin{equation*} 30~\mu\textrm{H} + 22.2~\mu\textrm{H} + 20~\mu\textrm{H} = 72.2~\mu\textrm{H} \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the 50 µH, 90 µH, and 72.2 µH inductors in parallel.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{1}{\frac{1}{50~\mu\textrm{H}} + \frac{1}{90~\mu\textrm{H}} + \frac{1}{72.2~\mu\textrm{H}}} = 22.24~\mu\textrm{H} \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the remaining two inductors in series to calculate the total equivalent inductance.
🔗
\begin{align*} L_{EQ} \amp= 80~\mu\textrm{H} + 22.24~\mu\textrm{H}\\ \amp= 102.2~\mu\textrm{H} \end{align*}
