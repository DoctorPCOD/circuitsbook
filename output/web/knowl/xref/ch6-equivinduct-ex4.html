Exercise 4.

For the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.9, calculate the equivalent inductance of the RL circuit. Then, calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor when the switch moves from position a to position b (\(R_{EQ,ab}\)), and for when the switch moves from position b to position a (\(R_{EQ,ba}\)).
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} L_{EQ} \amp= 2.89~\textrm{mH}\\ R_{EQ,ab} \amp= 690~\Omega\\ R_{EQ,ba} \amp= 676~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Start by combining the two series inductors. The equivalent inductance of the two series inductors is
🔗
\begin{equation*} 5.6~\textrm{mH} + 5.6~\textrm{mH} = 11.2~\textrm{mH}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the two parallel inductors. The equivalent inductance is equal to
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(11.2~\textrm{mH})(3.9~\textrm{mH})}{11.2~\textrm{mH} + 3.9~\textrm{mH}} = 2.89~\textrm{mH}\text{.} \end{equation*}

The equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor when the switch moves to position b can be analyzed with the switch in position b. All circuit elements disconnected from the circuit have been removed from the diagram.

The voltage source should be deactivated by replacing it with a short. The switch will also be replaced by a short.

🔗
