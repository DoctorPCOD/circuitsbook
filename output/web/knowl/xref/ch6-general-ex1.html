Exercise 21.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.82. The switch is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \left[3.14~\textrm{mA} - 0.64~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-35000t}\right]~u(t) + 2.5~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= 5.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-35000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Combine the two series resistors.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{(2~\textrm{k}\Omega)(10~\textrm{k}\Omega)}{2~\textrm{k}\Omega+10~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.67~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= 15~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{1.67~\textrm{k}\Omega}{10~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 2.5~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Perform source transformation on both sources.
🔗
The 15 mA current source becomes
🔗
\begin{equation*} (15~\textrm{mA})(2~\textrm{k}\Omega) = 30~\textrm{V}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The 24 V source becomes
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{24~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 6~\textrm{mA}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the series resistors.
🔗
Convert the voltage source to a current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{30~\textrm{V}}{6~\textrm{k}\Omega} = 5~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two current sources are in parallel and can be combined.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{1}{\frac{1}{6~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{1}{6~\textrm{k}\Omega} + \frac{1}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}}\\ \amp= 1.714~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= 11~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{1.71~\textrm{k}\Omega}{6~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 3.14~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\text{.}\) Deactivate the sources to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= (2~\textrm{k}\Omega+4~\textrm{k}\Omega)//4~\textrm{k}\Omega+6~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 6~\textrm{k}\Omega//4~\textrm{k}\Omega+6~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 2.4~\textrm{k}\Omega+6~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 8.4~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.5) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{.24~\textrm{H}}{8400~\Omega}\\ \amp= 2.86\times10^{-5}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.16) to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[3.14~\textrm{mA} - 0.64~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-35000t}\right]~u(t) + 2.5~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.17) to calculate \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (8.4~\textrm{k}\Omega)\left[(3.14~\textrm{mA} - 2.5~\textrm{mA})~\textrm{e}^{-35000t}\right]~u(t)\\ \amp= 5.4~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-35000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
