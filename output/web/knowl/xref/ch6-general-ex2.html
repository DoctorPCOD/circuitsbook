Exercise 22.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.91. Switch S1 is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds. Switch S2 is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \left[ 5~\textrm{mA} - 3~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}\right]~u(t) + 2~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= 6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
First, simplify the circuit by combining the 5 kΩ and 20 kΩ resistors in parallel.
🔗
Draw the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short. Switch S1 is closed and switch S2 is open.
🔗
The 4 kΩ resistor is shorted by the inductor and can be removed from the circuit.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{8~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 2~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short. Switch S1 is open and switch S2 is closed.
🔗
The 5 kΩ and 20 kΩ resistors are shorted by the inductor and can be removed from the circuit.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}}{4~\textrm{k}\Omega}\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) and deactivate the source to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= 4~\textrm{k}\Omega//4~\textrm{k}\Omega\\ \amp= 2~\textrm{k}\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.5) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{0.1~\textrm{H}}{2000~\Omega}\\ \amp= 5\times10^{-5}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.16) to calculate \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[ 5~\textrm{mA} - 3~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}\right]~u(t) + 2~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.17) to calculate \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (2~\textrm{k}\Omega)(5~\textrm{mA}-2~\textrm{mA})~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}~u(t)\\ \amp= (2~\textrm{k}\Omega)(3~\textrm{mA})~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}~u(t)\\ \amp= 6~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-20000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
