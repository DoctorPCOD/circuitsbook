Exercise 25.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.110. The switch has been open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds. The parameter values are \(I_{S1} = 5.5~\textrm{mA}\text{,}\) \(I_{S2} = 2.6~\textrm{mA}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 68~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 20~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_3 = 14~\Omega\text{,}\) \(L = 18~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) and \(k_i = 0.2\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \left(6.42~\textrm{mA} - 2.17~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-784t}\right)~u(t) + 4.25~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= 30.6~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-784t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit as seen by the current source.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{(68~\Omega)(20~\Omega)}{68~\Omega + 20~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{1360~\Omega^2}{88~\Omega}\\ \amp= 15.4545~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= 5.5~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{15.4545~\Omega}{20~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 4.25~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit in the final steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Resistor \(R_3\) is shorted out and can be removed from the circuit. In addition, the two current sources on the right-hand side of the circuit can be combined into one source.
🔗
Source transform \(I_{S1}\) into a voltage source of value \(R_1I_{S1}\text{.}\)
🔗
Combine the two resistors in series and then perform another source transformation back to a current source.
🔗
The \(R_1 + R_2\) resistor is shorted out.
🔗
Because the controlling current \(i_X(\infty)\) is situated between the two current sources, the two current sources cannot be added together, even though they are in parallel. Instead, use KCL twice, once at each node, to obtain two equations in terms of the two unknowns \(i(\infty)\) and \(i_X(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1 + R_2} \amp= i(\infty) + i_X(\infty)\tag{6.6.14}\\ i_X(\infty) + k_ii_X(\infty) + I_{S2} \amp= 0\tag{6.6.15} \end{align}
🔗
Solve (6.6.15) for \(i_X(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} (1+k_i)i_X(\infty) \amp= -I_{S2}\tag{6.6.16}\\ i_X(\infty) \amp= -\frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\tag{6.6.17} \end{align}
🔗
Plug (6.6.17) into (6.6.14) and solve for \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1 + R_2} \amp= i(\infty) - \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\\ i(\infty) \amp= \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1 + R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug in numerical quantities to calculate that \(i(\infty) = 6.4167~\textrm{mA}\text{.}\)
🔗
There are two methods that can be used to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor. The first method that will be used is to derive the Thévenin equivalent circuit as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Source transform \(I_{S1}\) into a voltage source.
🔗
Combine the two series resistors and transform back into a current source.
🔗
For the same reason as above, the two current sources cannot be combined into a single current source. The open-circuit voltage is the voltage that drops over either \(R_1 + R_2\) or \(R_3\text{.}\) The current through these two branches will therefore be equal to \(V_{OC}/(R_1+R_2)\) and \(V_{OC}/R_3\text{,}\) respectively. Use KCL at the two nodes between \(i_X(t)\) to obtain two equations in terms of \(V_{OC}\) and \(i_x(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{R_1+R_2} + i_X(t)\tag{6.6.18}\\ i_X(t) + I_{S2} + k_ii_X(t) \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3}\tag{6.6.19} \end{align}
🔗
Solve (6.6.19) for \(i_X(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} i_X(t) + k_ii_X(t) \amp= -I_{S2} + \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3}\tag{6.6.20}\\ (1+k_i)i_X(t) \amp= -I_{S2} + \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3}\tag{6.6.21}\\ i_X(t) \amp= \frac{-I_{S2}}{1+k_i} + \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3(1+k_i)}\tag{6.6.22} \end{align}
🔗
Plug (6.6.22) into (6.6.18) and reduce.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{R_1+R_2} - \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} + \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3(1+k_i)}\\ \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{V_{OC}}{R_3(1+k_i)} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug numerical values into the equation and solve for \(V_{OC}\text{.}\) Units, which are not explicitly shown, are mV, mA, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{align*} 4.25 + 2.1667 \amp= \frac{V_{OC}}{88} + \frac{V_{OC}}{16.8}\\ 16.4167 \amp= \frac{16.8V_{OC} + 88V_{OC}}{1478.4}\\ 9486.4 \amp= 104.8V_{OC}\\ V_{OC} \amp= 90.519~\textrm{mV} \end{align*}
🔗
Short the two terminals to calculate the short-circuit current.
🔗
Both parallel resistors are shorted and can be removed from the circuit.
🔗
Branch current \(i_X(t)\) must be equal to the negative value of the source \(I_{S2} + k_ii_X(t)\text{.}\) Use this to derive an equation for \(i_X(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} i_X(t) \amp= -I_{S2} - k_ii_X(t)\tag{6.6.23}\\ i_X(t) + k_ii_X(t) \amp= -I_{S2}\tag{6.6.24}\\ (1+K_i)i_X(t) \amp= -I_{S2}\tag{6.6.25}\\ i_X(t) \amp= \frac{-I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\tag{6.6.26} \end{align}
🔗
Use KCL at either node to obtain an equation relating \(I_{SC}\) and \(i_X(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} = I_{SC} + i_X(t)\tag{6.6.27} \end{equation}
🔗
Plug (6.6.26) into (6.6.27) and solve for \(I_{SC}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} \amp= I_{SC} - \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\\ I_{SC} \amp= \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug in numerical quantities to solve for \(I_SC\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{SC} \amp= 4.25~\textrm{mA} + 2.1667~\textrm{mA}\\ \amp= 6.4167~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the Thévenin equivalent resistance, which is equal to the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{90.519~\textrm{mV}}{6.4167~\textrm{mA}}\\ \amp= 14.1069~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.4) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{0.018~\textrm{H}}{14.1069~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0013~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
The second method to solve this circuit is to derive the differential equation.
🔗
Perform KCL at both nodes surrounding \(i_X(t)\) to obtain two equations.
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R_1+R_2} + i(t) + i_X(t)\tag{6.6.28}\\ i_X(t) + I_{S2} + k_ii_X(t) \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R_3}\tag{6.6.29} \end{align}
🔗
Solve (6.6.29) for \(i_X(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} i_X(t) (1+k_i) \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R_3} - I_{S2}\tag{6.6.30}\\ i_X(t) \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R_3(1+k_i)} - \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\tag{6.6.31} \end{align}
🔗
Plug (6.6.31) into (6.6.28) and combine like terms.
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R_1+R_2} + i(t) + \frac{v(t)}{R_3(1+k_i)} - \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}\tag{6.6.32}\\ \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} \amp= \left(\frac{1}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3(1+k_i)}\right)v(t) + i(t)\tag{6.6.33} \end{align}
🔗
The relationship between \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) is defined by (6.3.1). Plug (6.3.1) into (6.6.33) and put into standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i} \amp= \left(\frac{1}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3(1+k_i)}\right)L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + i(t)\\ \frac{\frac{R_1I_{S1}}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{I_{S2}}{1+k_i}}{L\left(\frac{1}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3(1+k_i)}\right)} \amp= \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{1}{L\left(\frac{1}{R_1+R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3(1+k_i)}\right)}\right) i(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Plug in numerical values, using base units for each quantity.
🔗
\begin{equation*} 5028.8 = \frac{di(t)}{dt} + 783.715i(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
This value agrees with the value calculated using the Thévenin equivalence method, as expected.
🔗
Use (6.5.13) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = \left(6.42~\textrm{mA} - 2.17~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-784t}\right)~u(t) + 4.25~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.14) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\) Recall that the equivalent resistance as seen by the capacitor is 14.1069 Ω. (If you did not solve for \(\tau\) using the Thévenin equivalence method, use (6.5.4) with the value of \(\tau\) derived from the differential equation and solve for \(R\text{.}\))
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 30.6~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-784t}~u(t) \end{equation*}
