Exercise 11.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.31. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-5000t}~u(t) + 5~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= -8~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-5000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short. The 700 Ω resistor is not connected to the circuit in this condition.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{6~\textrm{V}}{300~\Omega+900~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.005~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Draw the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= 700~\Omega+900~\Omega\\ \amp= 1600~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.4) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{L}{R}\\ \amp= \frac{0.32~\textrm{H}}{1600~\Omega}\\ \amp= 2\times10^{-4}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.2) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 5~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-5000t}~u(t) + 5~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.3) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= -(1600~\Omega)(0.005~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-5000t}~u(t))\\ \amp= -8~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-5000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
