Exercise 13.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.39. The switch has been open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 1.13~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-30000000t}~u(t) + 1.13~\textrm{A}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= -67.9~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-30000000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{120~\textrm{V}}{30~\Omega + 20~\Omega + 16~\Omega + 40~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{120~\textrm{V}}{106~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.13~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance seen by the inductor.
🔗
The voltage source, 30 Ω resistor, and 16 Ω resistor are shorted out of the circuit and can be removed.
🔗
The equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor is equal to the series combination of both resistors, which is 60 Ω. Use (6.5.4) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{2 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{H}}{60~\Omega}\\ \amp= 3.33 \times 10^{-8}~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.2) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 1.13~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-30000000t}~u(t) + 1.13~\textrm{A}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.3) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= -(60~\Omega)(1.13~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-30000000t}~u(t))\\ \amp= -67.9~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-30000000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
