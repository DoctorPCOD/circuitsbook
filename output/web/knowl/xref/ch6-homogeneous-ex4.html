Exercise 14.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.43. The switch has been closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= -13.8~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-75}~u(t) + 27.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= -0.831~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-75}~u(t) + 1.66~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short. Because the current through the resistor may be discontinuous with time, both \(i(0^-)\) and \(i_L(0)\) should be calculated.
🔗
Perform source transformation to convert the voltage source into a current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{1.8~\textrm{V}}{30~\Omega} = 0.06~\textrm{A} = 60~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{1}{\frac{1}{30~\Omega} + \frac{1}{20~\Omega} + \frac{1}{40~\Omega}}\\ \amp= 9.23~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(0^-)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0^-) \amp= 60~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{9.23~\Omega}{20~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 27.7~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i_L(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i_L(0) \amp= 60~\textrm{mA}\left(\frac{9.23~\Omega}{40~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 13.8~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\) to calculate \(i(0^+)\text{.}\) The inductor can be treated as a current source with a value of \(i_L(0)\text{.}\) The voltage source and 30 Ω resistor are disconnected from the circuit at this time.
🔗
Due to KCL, \(i(0^+)\) must be equal to −13.8 mA.
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor. The voltage source and 30 Ω resistor are disconnected from the circuit at this time.
🔗
The equivalent resistance is equal to the series combination of both resistors, which is 60 Ω. Use (6.5.5) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{0.8~\textrm{H}}{60~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.013~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.4) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = -13.8~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-75}~u(t) + 27.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (0.06~\textrm{k}\Omega)(-13.8~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-75}~u(t) + 27.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t))\\ \amp= -0.831~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-75}~u(t) + 1.66~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{align*}
