Exercise 15.

Derive equations for \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 6.6.48. The switch has been closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds. The parameter values are \(r_m = 8~\textrm{V/A}\text{,}\) \(L = 6~\textrm{H}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 1~\Omega\text{,}\) and \(R_2 = 4~\Omega\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= 40~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-1.2t}~u(t) + 40~\textrm{mA}~u(-t)\\ v(t) \amp= -288~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-1.2t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in the initial steady-state condition. The inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
The CCVS and resistors \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are shorted out of the circuit and can be removed.
🔗
Perform source transformation to convert the voltage source into a current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{0.4~\textrm{V}}{1~\Omega} = 0.4~\textrm{A} \end{equation*}
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= \frac{1}{\frac{1}{1~\Omega} + \frac{1}{2~\Omega} + \frac{1}{6~\Omega}}\\ \amp= 0.6~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= 0.4~\textrm{A}\frac{0.6~\Omega}{6~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.04~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 40~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t = 0^+\text{.}\) The voltage source, 1 Ω resistor, 2 Ω resistor, and 6 Ω resistor are disconnected from the circuit.
🔗
There are two methods that can be used to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor. The first method that will be used is to derive the Thévenin equivalent circuit as seen by the inductor.
🔗
Use the test source method to calculate the Thévenin equivalent resistance.
🔗
Use KVL around the left-hand mesh to derive an equation for \(V_{TEST}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} V_{TEST} \amp= r_mi_X(t) + R_1i_X(t)\\ \amp= (r_m + R_1)i_X(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting all three circuit branches to derive an equation for \(I_{TEST}\text{.}\) (Note that the voltage dropped over \(R_2\) is equal to the voltage dropped over \(R_1\text{,}\) which is equal to \(R_1i_X(t)\text{.}\))
🔗
\begin{align*} I_{TEST} \amp= i_X(t) + \frac{R_1}{R_2} i_X(t)\\ \amp= \left(1 + \frac{R_1}{R_2}\right)i_X(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the Thévenin equivalent resistance.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{TH} \amp= \frac{V_{TEST}}{I_{TEST}}\\ \amp= \frac{(r_m + R_1)i_X(t)}{\left(1 + \frac{R_1}{R_2}\right)i_X(t)}\\ \amp= \frac{r_m + R_1}{1 + \frac{R_1}{R_2}}\\ \amp= 7.2~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.5) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= \frac{6~\textrm{H}}{7.2~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.833~\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
The second method that can be used to solve the circuit is to derive a differential equation.
🔗
Use KVL around the left-hand mesh.
🔗
\begin{equation} L\frac{di(t)}{dt} = r_mi_X(t) + R_1i_X(t)\tag{6.6.1} \end{equation}
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting all three circuit branches. (Note that the voltage dropped over \(R_2\) is equal to the voltage dropped over \(R_1\text{,}\) which is equal to \(R_1i_X(t)\text{.}\))
🔗
\begin{equation} i(t) + i_X(t) + \frac{R_1}{R_2}i_X(t) = 0\tag{6.6.2} \end{equation}
🔗
Solve (6.6.2) for \(i_X(t)\) and simplify.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= -i_X(t) - \frac{R_1}{R_2}i_X(t)\\ \amp= -i_X(t) \left(1 + \frac{R_1}{R_2}\right)\\ i_X(t) \amp= -\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{R_1}{R_2}}\right)i(t)\\ \amp= -\left(\frac{1}{\frac{R_1+R_2}{R_2}}\right)i(t)\\ \amp= -\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\right)i(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Plug the simplified equation for \(i_X(t)\) into (6.6.1) and reduce to standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} L\frac{di(t)}{dt} \amp= -(r_m + R_1)\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\right)i(t)\\ 0 \amp= L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + (r_m + R_1)\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\right)i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{(r_m + R_1)\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\right)}{L}i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{di(t)}{dt} + 1.2i(t) \end{align*}
🔗
This yields the same result as the Thévenin equivalent circuit method, as expected. If we had not already derived the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor, we could use (6.5.5) to solve for \(R\text{.}\)
🔗
Use (6.5.4) to derive an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 40~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-1.2t}~u(t) + 40~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (6.5.6) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= -(7.2~\Omega)(40~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-1.2}~u(t))\\ \amp= -288~\textrm{mV}~\textrm{e}^{-1.2t}~u(t) \end{align*}
