Exercise 10.

Figure 6.6.30 depicts \(i(t)\) for a 56 µH inductor. Calculate \(\tau\text{,}\) \(R_{EQ}\) as seen by the inductor, and an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} \tau \amp= 2.89~\mu\textrm{s}\\ R_{EQ} \amp= 19.4~\Omega\\ v(t) \amp= -3.88~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-347000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the known coordinate (2 µs, 500 mA) using (6.5.16) to calculate \(\tau\text{.}\) (Because a unit of µs is used for the variable \(t\text{,}\) the unit of \(\tau\) in the calculation will also be µs.) Only the time period where \(t \geq 0\) will be considered.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \left(400~\textrm{mA} + 200~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-t/\tau}\right)\\ i(2) = 500~\textrm{mA} \amp= \left(400~\textrm{mA} + 200~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-2/\tau}\right)\\ 100~\textrm{mA} \amp= 200~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-2/\tau}\\ 0.5 \amp= \textrm{e}^{-2/\tau}\\ \ln{0.5} \amp= \ln{\textrm{e}^{-2/\tau}}\\ -0.6931 \amp= \frac{-2}{\tau}\\ \tau \amp= \frac{2}{0.6931}\\ \amp= 2.89~\mu\textrm{s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.5) to calculate the equivalent resistance as seen by the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} R \amp= \frac{L}{\tau}\\ \amp= \frac{56 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{H}}{2.89\times 10^{-6}~\textrm{s}}\\ \amp= 19.4~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (6.5.17) to derive an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= (0.0194~\textrm{k}\Omega)\left(-200~\textrm{mA}~\textrm{e}^{-347000t}\right)~u(t)\\ \amp= -3.88~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-347000t}~u(t) \end{align*}
