Exercise 19.

Derive the differential equation in terms of \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.66. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. Assume that the op-amps are sufficiently sourced such that the outputs will never saturate. The component values are \(V_{S1} = 0.6~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(V_{S2} = 1.4~\textrm{V}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 1~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_{F1} = 2~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 2.2~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_{F2} = 4.4~\textrm{k}\Omega\text{,}\) \(C_1 = 330~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(C_2 = 470~\mu\textrm{F}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[5.6~\textrm{V} - 4.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.484t} + 1.5~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1.515t}\right]~u(t) + 2.4~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state configuration. Both capacitors can be replaced by opens.
🔗
This circuit consists of two cascading inverting op-amps. Use (4.5.2) to calculate \(v_X(0)\) and use (4.5.4) and (4.5.2) to calculate \(v(0)\text{.}\) Both of these voltages are continuous with time.
🔗
\begin{align*} v_X(0) \amp= (0.6~\textrm{V})\left(-\frac{2~\textrm{K}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (0.6~\textrm{V})(-2)\\ \amp= -1.2~\textrm{V}\\ v(0) \amp= (0.6~\textrm{V})\left(-\frac{2~\textrm{K}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(-\frac{4.4~\textrm{K}\Omega}{2.2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (0.6~\textrm{V})(-2)(-2)\\ \amp= 2.4~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit in its final steady-state configuration to calculate the particular solution, \(v(\infty)\text{.}\) Both capacitors can be replaced by opens.
🔗
This circuit consists of two cascading inverting op-amps. Use (4.5.4) and (4.5.2) to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= (1.4~\textrm{V})\left(-\frac{2~\textrm{K}\Omega}{1~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\left(-\frac{4.4~\textrm{K}\Omega}{2.2~\textrm{k}\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (1.4~\textrm{V})(-2)(-2)\\ \amp= 5.6~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to derive the differential equation.
🔗
Perform KCL at the inverting input to the left-hand op-amp to obtain one equation.
🔗
\begin{equation} \frac{V_{S2}}{R_1} = -C_1\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} - \frac{v_X(t)}{R_{F1}}\tag{7.8.42} \end{equation}
🔗
Perform KCL at the inverting input to the right-hand op-amp to obtain the second equation. Solve for \(v_X(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{v_X(t)}{R_2} \amp= -C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} - \frac{v(t)}{R_{F2}}\tag{7.8.43}\\ v_X(t) \amp= -R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} - \frac{R_2}{R_{F2}}v(t)\tag{7.8.44} \end{align}
🔗
Plug (7.8.44) into (7.8.42) to obtain a differential equation in terms of \(v(t)\text{.}\) Then, reduce to standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{V_{S2}}{R_1} \amp= -C_1\frac{d}{dt}\left(-R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} - \frac{R_2}{R_{F2}}v(t)\right) - \frac{1}{R_{F1}}\left(-R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} - \frac{R_2}{R_{F2}}v(t)\right)\\ \amp= R_2C_1C_2\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \frac{C_1R_2}{R_{F2}}\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{C_2R_2}{R_{F1}}\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{R_2}{R_{F1}R_{F2}}v(t)\\ \frac{V_{S2}}{R_1R_2C_1C_2} \amp= \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{1}{C_2R_{F2}} + \frac{1}{C_1R_{F1}}\right)\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{1}{C_1C_2R_{F1}R_{F2}}\right)v(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Plug in component values using base units.
🔗
\begin{equation*} 4.1029 = \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + 1.9987\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + 0.7327v(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
Use (7.2.1) to calculate the damping parameter, and use (7.2.2) to calculate the resonant frequency.
🔗
\begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{1.9987}{2}\\ \amp= 0.9994~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= \sqrt{0.7327}\\ \amp= 0.8560~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
This circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -0.9994 + \sqrt{0.9994^2 - 0.8560^2}\\ \amp= -0.4836\\ s_2 \amp= -0.9994 - \sqrt{0.9994^2 - 0.8560^2}\\ \amp= -1.5152 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.8.44) to calculate \(v'(0^+)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v'(0^+) \amp= \frac{v_X(0^+) + \frac{R_2}{R_{F2}}v(0^+)}{-R_2C_2}\\ \amp= \frac{-1.2~\textrm{V} + \frac{2.2~\textrm{k}\Omega}{4.4~\textrm{k}\Omega}(2.4~\textrm{V})}{-(2.2~\textrm{k}\Omega)(470~\mu\textrm{F})}\\ \amp= 0~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.6.4) and (7.6.5) to calculate the coefficients of the equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{-1.5152(2.4-5.6)}{-1.5152 - (-0.4836)}\\ \amp= -4.7~\textrm{V}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{0.4836(2.4-5.6)}{-1.5152 - (-0.4836)}\\ \amp= 1.5~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug everything into (7.6.3) to obtain the equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[5.6~\textrm{V} - 4.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.484t} + 1.5~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-1.515t}\right]~u(t) + 2.4~\textrm{V}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
