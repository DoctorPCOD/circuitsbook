Exercise 9.

Derive an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.29. The switch is in position a for a very long time before moving to position b at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are: \(R_D = 20~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R = 10~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C = 400~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) and \(L = 100~\textrm{mH}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \textrm{e}^{-113 t} \left[ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~\cos{158 t} + 47.6~\textrm{mA}~\sin{158 t} \right]u(t)\\ \amp+ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{4~\textrm{V}}{50~\Omega + 10~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0667~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 66.6667~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(v_C(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v_C(0) \amp= 4~\textrm{V}\left(\frac{10~\Omega}{10~\Omega+50~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 0.6667~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
🔗
Use KCL at the node connecting \(R_D\text{,}\) \(C\text{,}\) and \(L\text{.}\) Use Ohm’s law to derive the current through resistor \(R_D\text{,}\) and (5.3.1) to derive the current through the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{equation} \frac{v_C(t)}{R_D} + C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} + i(t) = 0\tag{7.8.10} \end{equation}
🔗
Use KVL around the right-hand loop of the circuit. Use (6.3.1) to derive the voltage dropped over the inductor.
🔗
\begin{equation} v_C(t) = L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\tag{7.8.11} \end{equation}
🔗
Plug (7.8.11) into (7.8.10) to obtain a differential equation in terms of \(i(t)\text{.}\) Then, reduce to standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} 0 \amp= \frac{1}{R_D}\left(L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\right) + C\frac{d}{dt}\left(L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t)\right) + i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{L}{R_D}\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{R}{R_D}i(t) + LC\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + RC\frac{di(t)}{dt} + i(t)\\ \amp= LC\frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{L}{R_D} + RC\right)\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R}{R_D}+1\right)i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{1}{R_DC}+\frac{R}{L}\right)\frac{di(t)}{dt} + \left(\frac{R}{LCR_D}+\frac{1}{LC}\right)i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{d^2i(t)}{dt^2} + 225\frac{di(t)}{dt} + 37500 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.2.1) and (7.2.2) to calculate the damping parameter and resonant frequency.
🔗
\begin{align*} \alpha \amp= 112.5~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= 193.6492~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
The circuit is underdamped. Use (7.2.5) to calculate the oscillation frequency.
🔗
\begin{align*} \beta \amp= \sqrt{(193.6492~\textrm{rad/s})^2 - (112.5~\textrm{Np/s})^2}\\ \amp= 157.6190~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.8.11) to calculate the initial condition of the first derivative of the current.
🔗
\begin{align*} v_C(0^+) \amp= Li'(0^+) + Ri(0^+)\\ Li'(0^+) \amp= Ri(0^+) - v_C(0^+)\\ i(0^+) \amp= \frac{Ri(0^+) - v_C(0^+)}{L}\\ \amp= \frac{(10~\Omega)(0.0667~\textrm{A}) - 0.6667~\textrm{V}}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= \frac{0.6667~\textrm{V} - 0.6667~\textrm{V}}{0.1~\textrm{H}}\\ \amp= 0~\textrm{A/s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.5.5) and (7.5.6) to calculate the coefficients.
🔗
\begin{align*} B_1 \amp= 0.0667~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 66.6667~\textrm{mA}\\ B_2 \amp= \frac{0~\textrm{A/s} + (112.5~\textrm{Np/s})(0.0667~\textrm{A})}{157.6190~\textrm{rad/s}}\\ \amp= 0.0476~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 47.5831~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug the values into (7.5.4) to obtain an equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(t) \amp= \textrm{e}^{-113 t} \left[ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~\cos{158 t} + 47.6~\textrm{mA}~\sin{158 t} \right]u(t)\\ \amp+ 66.7~\textrm{mA}~u(-t) \end{align*}
