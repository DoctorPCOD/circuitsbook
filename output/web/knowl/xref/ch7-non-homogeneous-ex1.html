Exercise 11.

Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.37. The switch is in position b for a very long time before moving to position a at a time of zero seconds. The component values are \(I_S = 200~\textrm{mA}\text{,}\) \(R_S = 100~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 20~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 40~\Omega\text{,}\) \(C_1 = 5~\textrm{mF}\text{,}\) and \(C_2 = 20~\textrm{mF}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[20~\textrm{V} -20.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.273t} + 0.739~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-7.64t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The initial voltage drops will both be zero as the circuit is not connected to a source in its initial steady-state configuration. Both of these values are continuous with time, as they are voltages measured over capacitors.
🔗
To calculate the particular solution, analyze the circuit in its final steady-state condition. Each capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Neither \(R_1\) nor \(R_2\) contribute to the circuit in this configuration. Both \(v_X(\infty)\) and \(v(\infty)\) are equal to each other. Use Ohm’s law to calculate this voltage.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= (0.2~\textrm{A})(100~\Omega)\\ \amp= 20~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to derive the differential equation.
🔗
Transform the current source to a voltage source.
🔗
Combine series resistors.
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting \(R_S+R_1\text{,}\) \(C_1\text{,}\) and \(R_2\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{I_SR_S-v_X(t)}{R_S+R_1} \amp= C_1\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} + C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\tag{7.8.16}\\ \frac{I_SR_S}{R_S+R_1} \amp= \frac{v_X(t)}{R_S+R_1} + C_1\frac{dv_X(t)}{dt} + C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\tag{7.8.17} \end{align}
🔗
Perform KVL around the right-hand loop of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{equation} v_X(t) = R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\tag{7.8.18} \end{equation}
🔗
Plus (7.8.18) into (7.8.17) and reduce to standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{I_SR_S}{R_S+R_1} \amp= \frac{1}{R_S+R_1}\left(R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\right)\\ \amp+ C_1\frac{d}{dt}\left(R_2C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + v(t)\right) + C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \amp= \frac{R_2C_2}{R_S+R_1}\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{R_S+R_1}v(t) + C_1C_2R_2\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2}\\ \amp+ C_1\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + C_2\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \amp= C_1C_2R_2\frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{R_2C_2}{R_S+R_1} + C_1 + C_2\right)\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \amp+ \frac{1}{R_S+R_1}v(t)\\ \frac{I_SR_S}{C_1C_2(R_S+R_1)R_2} \amp= \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{1}{(R_S+R_1)C_1} + \frac{1}{R_2C_2} + \frac{1}{R_2C_1}\right)\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ \amp+ \frac{1}{C_1C_2(R_S+R_1)R_2}v(t)\\ 41.667 \amp= \frac{d^2v(t)}{dt^2} + 7.9167\frac{dv(t)}{dt} + 2.0833 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.2.1) to calculate the damping parameter and use (7.2.2) to calculate the resonant frequency.
🔗
\begin{align*} \alpha \amp= 3.9583~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= 1.4434~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
(7.8.18) can be used to calculate \(v'(0^+)\text{.}\) Because both \(v(0^+)\) and \(v_X(0^+)\) are zero, \(v'(0^+)\) will also be zero.
🔗
The circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -3.9583 + \sqrt{3.9583^2 - 1.4434^2}\\ \amp= -0.2725\\ s_2 \amp= -3.9583 - \sqrt{3.9583^2 - 1.4434^2}\\ \amp= -7.6441 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.6.4) and (7.6.5) to calculate the coefficients of the equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{(0-20)(-7.6441)}{-7.6441-(-0.2725)}\\ \amp= -20.7394~\textrm{V}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{- (0-20)(-0.2725)}{-7.6441-(-0.2725)}\\ \amp= 0.7394~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug these values into (7.6.3) to derive the equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[20~\textrm{V} -20.7~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-0.273t} + 0.739~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-7.64t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
