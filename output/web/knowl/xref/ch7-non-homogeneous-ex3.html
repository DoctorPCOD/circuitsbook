Exercise 13.

Derive an equation for \(i(t)\) given the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.46. Switch \(S_1\) is closed for a very long time before opening at a time of zero seconds. Switch \(S_2\) is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds. The component values are \(L = 50~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) \(C = 20~\mu\textrm{F}\text{,}\) \(R_1 = 10~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R_2 = 20~\Omega\text{,}\) and \(V_S = 15~\textrm{V}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[0.5~\textrm{A} + 0.97~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-782t} - 0.27~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-1920t}\right]~u(t) + 1.2~\textrm{A}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Analyze the circuit in its initial steady-state condition. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(0)\text{.}\) This value is continuous with time.
🔗
\begin{align*} i(0) \amp= \frac{6~\textrm{V}}{5~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.2~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
\(v_X(0)\) will be equal to zero, as it’s measured over the inductor (short). This value is also continuous with time.
🔗
Draw the circuit in the final steady-state condition to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{,}\) the particular solution. The capacitor can be replaced by an open and the inductor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(i(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i(\infty) \amp= \frac{15~\textrm{V}}{30~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.5~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\) to derive the differential equation.
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting \(R_1\text{,}\) \(R_2\text{,}\) and \(C\text{.}\) Collect like terms to simplify.
🔗
\begin{align} \frac{V_S - v_X(t)}{R_2} \amp= C\frac{d}{dt}v_X(t) + i(t)\tag{7.8.22}\\ \frac{V_S}{R_2} \amp= \left[\frac{1}{R_2} + C\frac{d}{dt}\right] v_X(t) + i(t)\tag{7.8.23} \end{align}
🔗
Perform KVL around the left loop of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{equation} v_X(t) = R_1i(t) + L\frac{d}{dt}i(t)\tag{7.8.24} \end{equation}
🔗
Plug (7.8.24) into (7.8.23) and reduce until the differential equation is in standard form.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{V_S}{R_2} \amp= \left[\frac{1}{R_2} + C\frac{d}{dt}\right]\left[R_1i(t) + L\frac{d}{dt}i(t)\right] v_X(t) + i(t)\\ \amp= \frac{R_1}{R_2}i(t) + \frac{L}{R_2}\frac{d}{dt}i(t) + R_1C\frac{d}{dt}i(t) + LC\frac{d^2}{dt^2}i(t) + i(t)\\ \amp= LC\frac{d^2}{dt^2}i(t) + \left[R_1C + \frac{L}{R_2}\right]\frac{d}{dt}i(t) + \left[\frac{R_1}{R_2} + 1\right]i(t)\\ \frac{V_S}{R_2LC} \amp= \frac{d^2}{dt^2}i(t) + \left[\frac{R_1}{L} + \frac{1}{R_2C}\right]\frac{d}{dt}i(t) + \left[\frac{R_1}{R_2LC} + \frac{1}{LC}\right]i(t)\\ 750000 \amp= \frac{d^2}{dt^2}i(t) + 2700\frac{d}{dt}i(t) + 1500000i(t) \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.2.1) to calculate the damping parameter and use (7.2.2) to calculate the resonant frequency.
🔗
\begin{align*} \alpha \amp= 1350~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= 1224.74~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
This circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -1350 + \sqrt{1350^2 - 1224.74^2}\\ \amp= -782.10\\ s_2 \amp= -1350 - \sqrt{1350^2 - 1224.74^2}\\ \amp= -1917.90 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.8.24) to calculate \(i'(0^+)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} i'(0^+) \amp= \frac{v_X(0^+) - R_1i(0^+)}{L}\\ \amp= -240~\textrm{A/s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.6.4) and (7.6.5) to calculate the coefficients of the equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{(1.2-0.5)(-1917.90)-(-240)}{-1917.90-(-782.10)}\\ \amp= 0.97~\textrm{A}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{-240 - (1.5-0.5)(-782.10)}{-1917.90-(-782.10)}\\ \amp= -0.27~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug everything into (7.6.3) to obtain the equation for \(i(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = \left[0.5~\textrm{A} + 0.97~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-782t} - 0.27~\textrm{A}~\textrm{e}^{-1920t}\right]~u(t) + 1.2~\textrm{A}~u(-t) \end{equation*}
