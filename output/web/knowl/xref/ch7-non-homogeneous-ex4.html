Exercise 14.

Derive an equation for \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 7.8.50. The switch is open for a very long time before closing at a time of zero seconds. The circuit parameters are: \(I_S = 660~\textrm{mA}\text{,}\) \(R_S = 33~\Omega\text{,}\) \(R = 15~\Omega\text{,}\) \(L = 1.8~\textrm{mH}\text{,}\) and \(C = 560~\mu\textrm{F}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[6.81~\textrm{V} - 6.81~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-176t} + 0.002~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-8210t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
In the initial steady-state configuration, there is no source connected to the circuit. Therefore, \(i_L(0)\) and \(v(0)\) will both be equal to zero. Both of these values are continuous with time.
🔗
Analyze the circuit in its final steady-state configuration to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{,}\) the particular solution. The inductor can be replaced by a short and the capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} R_{EQ} \amp= 33~\Omega // 15~\Omega\\ \amp= 10.3125~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(v(\infty)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} v(\infty) \amp= (0.6~\textrm{A})(10.3125~\Omega)\\ \amp= 6.8063~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(t=0^+\text{.}\)
🔗
This circuit is mostly equivalent to a parallel RLC circuit with parasitic resistance. (Instead of measuring \(i_L(t)\text{,}\) we are measuring \(v(t)\text{.}\) However, this will not change the characteristic equation of this circuit.) We have already derived the differential equation for this circuit in Subsection 7.4.3. Use (7.4.10) to calculate the damping parameter and use (7.4.11) to calculate the resonant frequency of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} \alpha \amp= \frac{1}{(2)(33~\Omega)(560 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})} + \frac{15~\Omega}{(2)(0.0018~\textrm{H})}\\ \amp= 4193.7~\textrm{Np/s}\\ \omega_0 \amp= \sqrt{\frac{1}{(0.0018~\textrm{H})(560 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})} + \frac{15~\Omega}{(33~\Omega)(0.0018~\textrm{H})(560 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F})}}\\ \amp= 1201.2~\textrm{rad/s} \end{align*}
🔗
This circuit is overdamped. Use (7.2.3) and (7.2.4) to calculate the roots of the characteristic equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} s_1 \amp= -4193.7 + \sqrt{4193.7^2 - 1201.2^2}\\ \amp= -175.7246\\ s_2 \amp= -4193.7 - \sqrt{4193.7^2 - 1201.2^2}\\ \amp= -8211.7 \end{align*}
🔗
Because we are solving for \(v(t)\) instead of current, we must perform KCL at either circuit node to obtain an equation to use to solve for \(v'(0^+)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} I_S \amp= \frac{v(t)}{R} + i_L(t) + C\frac{dv(t)}{dt}\\ I_S \amp= \frac{v(0^+)}{R} + i_L(0^+) + Cv'(0^+)\\ v'(0^+) \amp= \frac{I_S - \frac{v(0^+)}{R} - i_L(0^+)}{C}\\ \amp= \frac{0.66~\textrm{A}}{560 \times 10^{-6}~\textrm{F}}\\ \amp= 1178.6~\textrm{V/s} \end{align*}
🔗
Use (7.6.4) and (7.6.5) to calculate the coefficients of the equation.
🔗
\begin{align*} A_1 \amp= \frac{-8211.7(0-6.8063) - 1178.6}{-8211.7 - (-175.7246)}\\ \amp= -6.8084~\textrm{V}\\ A_2 \amp= \frac{1178.6 - (-175.7246)(0-6.8063)}{-8211.7 - (-175.7246)}\\ \amp= 0.0022~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Plug everything into (7.6.3) to obtain an equation for \(v(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = \left[6.81~\textrm{V} - 6.81~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-176t} + 0.002~\textrm{V}~\textrm{e}^{-8210t}\right]~u(t) \end{equation*}
