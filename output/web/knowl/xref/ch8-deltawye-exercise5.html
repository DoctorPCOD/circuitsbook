Exercise 15.

Calculate the equivalent impedance between nodes a and b of the circuit shown in Figure 8.4.28. The frequency of operation is 50 Hz.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} 53.1 -j22.9~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of each capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{1}{j(2\pi 50)(60 \times 10^{-6})} \amp= -j53.0516~\Omega\\ \frac{1}{j(2\pi 50)(50 \times 10^{-6})} \amp= -j63.6620~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (8.2.5) to calculate the impedance of each inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} j(2\pi 50)(0.06) \amp= j18.8496~\Omega\\ j(2\pi 50)(0.08) \amp= j25.1327~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
The 10 Ω resistor is connected in series with the −j53 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} (10~\Omega) + (-j53.0516~\Omega) = 10 -j53.0516~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
A wye to delta transform will be done to solve this circuit. (A delta to wye transform can also be used. Feel free to try it out for extra practice.) The wye impedances are highlighted in red below, with the wye nodes labeled.
🔗
Use (8.3.10), (8.3.11), and (8.3.12) to calculate the delta impedances.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_1} \amp= \frac{(j18.8~\Omega)(j25.1~\Omega)+(j18.8~\Omega)(-j63.7~\Omega)+(j25.1~\Omega)(-j63.7~\Omega)}{j18.8496~\Omega}\\ \amp= \frac{2326.3~\Omega}{j18.8496~\Omega}\\ \amp= -j123.41~\Omega\\ \vec{Z_2} \amp= \frac{2326.3~\Omega}{-j63.6620~\Omega}\\ \amp= j36.5408~\Omega\\ \vec{Z_3} \amp= \frac{2326.3~\Omega}{j25.1327~\Omega}\\ \amp= -j92.5589~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
The 10−j53.05 Ω impedance is connected in parallel with the −j92.56 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(10 -j53.0516~\Omega)(-j92.5589~\Omega)}{(10 -j53.0516~\Omega)+(-j92.5589~\Omega)} = 4.0217 -j33.9990~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
The 20 Ω resistor is connected in parallel with the j36.54 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(20~\Omega)(j36.5408~\Omega)}{(20~\Omega)+(j36.5408~\Omega)} = 15.3897 + j8.4233~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
The 4−j34 Ω impedance is connected in series with the 15.4+j8.42 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} (4.0217 -j33.9990~\Omega) + (15.3897 + j8.4233~\Omega) = 19.4113 - j25.5758~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
The 19.4−j25.6 Ω impedance is connected in parallel with the −j123.41 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(19.4113 - j25.5758~\Omega)(-j123.41~\Omega)}{(19.4113 - j25.5758~\Omega)+(-j123.41~\Omega)} = 13.0966 - j22.8917~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
The 40 Ω resistor is connected in series with the 13.1−j22.89 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_{EQ}} \amp= (40~\Omega) + (13.0966 - j22.8917~\Omega)\\ \amp= 53.1 -j22.9~\Omega \end{align*}
