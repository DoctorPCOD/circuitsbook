Example 9.4.1 Active first-order low-pass filter.

An active first-order low-pass filter is shown below. Calculate the cutoff frequency of the filter. Then, confirm the filter type of the circuit.
🔗
The transfer function for this circuit was derived in Example 9.2.5 and is given below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} H(j\omega) = \frac{-\frac{1}{R_1C}}{j\omega + \frac{1}{R_2C}} \end{equation*}
🔗
To calculate the cutoff frequency, divide the transfer function by the gain of the circuit in the pass-band. Because this is a low-pass filter, the pass-band encompasses low frequencies. At low frequencies, the capacitor can be replaced by an open, and the op-amp is an inverting op-amp with
🔗
\begin{equation*} G = -\frac{R_2}{R_1}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
The cutoff frequency can now be calculated.
🔗
\begin{align*} |H(j\omega)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \amp= \frac{\frac{1}{R_2C}}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2 + \omega_c^2}}\\ \frac{1}{2} \amp= \frac{\left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2}{\left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2 + \omega_c^2}\\ \left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2 + \omega_c^2 \amp= 2\left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2\\ \omega_c^2 \amp= \left(\frac{1}{R_2C}\right)^2\\ \omega_c \amp= \frac{1}{R_2C} \end{align*}
🔗
To determine the filter type, first analyze the circuit at \(\omega=0\) to calculate \(v_{out}(0)\text{.}\) The capacitor can be replaced by an open.
🔗
Use (4.6.2) to calculate \(v_{out}(0)\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} v_{out}(0) = -\frac{R_2}{R_1}v_{in} \end{equation*}
🔗
Analyze the circuit at \(\omega\rightarrow\infty\) to calculate \(v_{out}(\infty)\text{.}\) The capacitor can be replaced by a short.
🔗
The output voltage is zero due to the virtual node between the non-inverting and inverting inputs, and the fact that the shorted capacitor turns this into a voltage follower circuit (refer to Subsection 4.5.1).
🔗
Consult Table 9.3.31. The circuit is a first-order, active, low-pass filter.
