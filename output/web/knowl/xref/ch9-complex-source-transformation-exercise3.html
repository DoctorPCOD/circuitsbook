Exercise 23.

Use source transformation to calculate \(\vec{I}\) for the circuit shown in Figure 9.6.72.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} 288-j60.4~\textrm{mA} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Convert both voltage sources into current sources.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_1} \amp= \frac{20~\textrm{V}}{6~\Omega}\\ \amp= 3.33~\textrm{A}\\ \vec{I_2} \amp= \frac{30~\textrm{V}}{-j7~\Omega}\\ \amp= j4.28~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
The 6 Ω resistor is connected in parallel with the −j5 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z'} \amp= \frac{(6~\Omega)(-j5~\Omega)}{(6~\Omega)+(-j5~\Omega)}\\ \amp= 2.4590 - j2.9508~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Convert the 3.33 A source to a voltage source.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_S} \amp= (3.33~\textrm{A})(2.46-j2.95~\Omega)\\ \amp= 8.20-j9.84~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
The 2.46−j2.95 Ω impedance is connected in series with the j4 Ω impedance.
🔗
Convert the voltage source back to a current source.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_S} \amp= \frac{8.20-j9.84~\textrm{V}}{2.46+j1.05~\Omega}\\ \amp= 1.38-j4.59~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Combine the current sources.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_{TOTAL}} \amp= (1.38-j4.59~\textrm{A})+(j4.28~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= 1.38-j0.30~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
The 2.46+j1.05 Ω impedance is connected in parallel with the −j7 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z''} \amp= \frac{(-j7~\Omega)(2.46+j1.05~\Omega)}{(-j7~\Omega)+(2.46+j1.05~\Omega)}\\ \amp= 2.91+j0.03~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate the equivalent impedance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_{EQ}} \amp= \frac{(2.91+j0.03~\Omega)(11~\Omega)}{(2.91+j0.03~\Omega)+(11~\Omega)}\\ \amp= 2.2989 + j0.0208~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(\vec{I}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I} \amp= (1.38-j0.30~\textrm{A})\left(\frac{j0.0208~\Omega}{11~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 0.29-j0.06~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 288-j60.4~\textrm{mA} \end{align*}
