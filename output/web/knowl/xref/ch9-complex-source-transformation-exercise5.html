Exercise 25.

Use source transformation to calculate \(v(t)\) for the circuit shown in Figure 9.6.85. The source voltage is \(v_{AC}(t) = 3~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi100t)\text{.}\) The proportionality constant of the VCCS has a unit of A/V.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 576~\textrm{mV}~\cos(2\pi100t - 53.9\pi/180) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of both capacitors.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{1}{j(2\pi100)(0.0016)} \amp= -j0.9947~\Omega\\ \frac{1}{j(2\pi100)(0.0003)} \amp= -j5.3052~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Convert the independent source to a phasor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_{AC}} \amp= 3~\cos{0^\circ} +j3~\sin{0^\circ}~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 3~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Convert the voltage source into a current source. Convert the VCCS into a VCVS.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{3~\textrm{V}}{2~\Omega} \amp= 1.5~\textrm{A}\\ (-(2~\textrm{A/V})\vec{V})(5~\Omega) \amp= -10\vec{V} \end{align*}
🔗
The 2 Ω resistor is connected in parallel with the −j0.995 Ω impedance. The 5 Ω resistor is connected in series with the −5.31 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(2~\Omega)(-j0.995~\Omega)}{(2~\Omega)+(-j0.995~\Omega)} = 0.3966 - j0.7975~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Convert the dependent source to a VCCS. The unit of the proportionality constant is A/V.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{10\vec{V}}{5-j5.31} = (0.94+j1.00)\vec{V} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two impedances are connected in parallel.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(0.3966 - j0.7975~\Omega)(5 - j5.3052~\Omega)}{(0.3966 - j0.7975~\Omega)+(5 - j5.3052~\Omega)} = 0.3774 - j0.7020~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Combine the two sources. (Note that the source is a current source, which may appear slightly unclear from the circuit diagram.)
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(\vec{V}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V} \amp= (0.38-j0.70)(1.5 - (0.94 + j1) \vec{V})\\ \amp= (0.57-j1.05) - (1.06-j0.28)\vec{V}\\ (2.06-j0.28)\vec{V} \amp= (0.57-j1.05)\\ \vec{V} \amp= \frac{(0.57-j1.05)}{(2.06-j0.28)}\\ \amp= 0.34-j0.47~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 339.57-j465.34~\textrm{mV}\\ \amp= 576.06~\textrm{mV} \angle -53.88^\circ \end{align*}
🔗
Convert to time-varying from.
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 576~\textrm{mV}~\cos(2\pi100t - 53.9\pi/180) \end{equation*}
