Exercise 18.

Calculate \(v(t)\) given the circuit diagram shown in Figure 9.6.45. The frequency of operation is 100 Hz.
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 434~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi 100t - 134\pi/180) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The 12 Ω resistor and j16 Ω impedance are connected in series.
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{V_1}\text{,}\) the voltage contributed by the 100−j50 V voltage source. Deactivate the current source.
🔗
Use the voltage divider rule to calculate \(\vec{V_1}\text{.}\) (Refer to Example 2.5.5 for information on solving a two-mesh voltage divider circuit.)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_1} \amp= (100-j50~\textrm{V})\left(\frac{j5~\Omega//(12+j6~\Omega)}{j5~\Omega//(12+j6~\Omega)+20~\Omega}\right)\left(\frac{12+j16~\Omega}{12+j6~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= (100-j50~\textrm{V})\left(\frac{1.13+j3.96~\Omega}{21.13+j3.96~\Omega}\right)\left(1.33+j0.67\right)\\ \amp= (100-j50~\textrm{V})(0.09+j0.17)\left(1.33+j0.67\right)\\ \amp= 14.29+j28.57~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{V_2}\text{,}\) the voltage contributed by the 30+j20 A current source. Deactivate the voltage source.
🔗
The 20 Ω resistor is connected in parallel with the j5 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V'} \amp= \frac{(20~\Omega)(j5~\Omega)}{(20~\Omega)+(j5~\Omega)}\\ \amp= 1.1765 + j4.7059~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate the equivalent impedance of the circuit as seen by the current source.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_{EQ}} \amp= \frac{((1.1765 + j4.7059~\Omega)+(12+j16~\Omega))(-j10~\Omega)}{((1.1765 + j4.7059~\Omega)+(12+j16~\Omega))+(-j10~\Omega)}\\ \amp= 4.5714 - j13.7143~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate the current through the branch containing the 12+j16 Ω impedance.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I} \amp= (30+j20~\textrm{A})\left(\frac{4.5714 - j13.7143}{13.18+j20.71~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= -2-j21.14~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(\vec{V_2}\text{.}\) Note that the polarity opposes the current source, which is why it is multiplied by negative one.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_2} \amp= -(12+j16~\Omega)(-2-j21.14~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= -314.29+j285.71~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{V}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V} \amp= \vec{V_1}+\vec{V_2}\\ \amp= (14.29+j28.57~\textrm{V}) + (-314.29+j285.71~\textrm{V})\\ \amp= -300 + 314.29~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 434.4830~\textrm{V} \angle 133.6678^\circ \end{align*}
🔗
Convert to time-varying form.
🔗
\begin{equation*} v(t) = 434~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi 100t - 134\pi/180) \end{equation*}
