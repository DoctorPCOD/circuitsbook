Exercise 19.

Calculate \(i(t)\) given the circuit diagram shown in Figure 9.6.50. The current source has a value of \(i_{AC}(t) = 5~\textrm{A}~\cos(2\pi2000t)\) and the voltage source has a value of \(v_{AC}(t) = 20~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi2000t + 90\pi/180)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 2.23~\textrm{A}~\cos(2\pi 2000t + 64.6\pi/180) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of both capacitors.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{1}{j(2\pi 2000)(20 \times 10^{-6})} \amp= -j3.9789~\Omega\\ \frac{1}{j(2\pi 2000)(10 \times 10^{-6})} \amp= -j7.9577~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (8.2.5) to calculate the impedance of the inductor.
🔗
\begin{equation*} j(2\pi2000)(330 \times 10^{-6}) = j3.8956~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Convert both sources to phasors.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_{AC}} \amp= 20~\cos{90^\circ} + j20~\sin{90^\circ}~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= j20~\textrm{V}\\ \vec{I_{AC}} \amp= 5~\cos{0^\circ} + j5~\sin{0^\circ}~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 5~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{I_1}\text{,}\) the current contributed by the voltage source. Deactivate the current source.
🔗
The −j7.96 Ω and −j3.98 Ω impedances are connected in series.
🔗
The 12 Ω resistor and −j11.9 Ω impedance are connected in parallel.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(12~\Omega)(-j11.9366~\Omega)}{(12~\Omega)+(-j11.9366~\Omega)} = 5.9682 - j6~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(\vec{I_1}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_1} \amp= \frac{j20~\textrm{V}}{(5.9682 - j6~\Omega)+(j3.8956~\Omega)}\\ \amp= -1.0509 + j2.9805~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{I_2}\text{,}\) the current contributed by the current source. Deactivate the voltage source. A new branch current called \(\vec{I_X}\) is introduced to assist in the analysis of this circuit.
🔗
The 12 Ω resistor is connected in parallel with the j3.8 Ω impedance. This will remove \(\vec{I_2}\) from the circuit. Once we calculate \(\vec{I_X}\text{,}\) we will come back to solve for \(\vec{I_2}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(12~\Omega)(j3.8956~\Omega)}{(12~\Omega)+(j3.8956~\Omega)} = 1.1441 + j3.5242~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
The −j7.96 Ω and 1.14+j3.52 Ω impedances are connected in series.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent impedance of the circuit as seen by the current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(1.1441 - j4.4336~\Omega)(-j3.9789~\Omega)}{(1.1441 - j4.4336~\Omega)+(-j3.9789~\Omega)} = 0.2513 - j2.1311~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(\vec{I_X}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_X} \amp= (5~\textrm{A})\left(\frac{0.2513 - j2.1311~\Omega}{1.1441 - j4.4336~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 2.3219 - 0.3158~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
The equivalent circuit consisting of the 12 Ω resistor and j3.9 Ω impedance is shown below.
🔗
Calculate the equivalent impedance of the circuit as seen by the current source.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(j3.8956~\Omega)(12~\Omega)}{(j3.8956~\Omega)+(12~\Omega)} = 1.1441 + j3.5242~\Omega \end{equation*}
🔗
Use the current divider rule to calculate \(\vec{I_2}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I_2} \amp= (2.3219 - 0.3158~\textrm{A})\left(\frac{1.1441 + j3.5242~\Omega}{j3.8956~\Omega}\right)\\ \amp= 2.0078 - j0.9676~\textrm{A} \end{align*}
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{I}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I} \amp= \vec{I_1}+\vec{I_2}\\ \amp= (-1.05+j2.98~\textrm{A}) + (2.01-j0.97~\textrm{A})\\ \amp= 0.9569 + j2.0130~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 2.2288~\textrm{A} \angle 64.5747^\circ \end{align*}
🔗
Convert to time-varying form.
🔗
\begin{equation*} i(t) = 2.23~\textrm{A}~\cos(2\pi 2000t + 64.6\pi/180) \end{equation*}
