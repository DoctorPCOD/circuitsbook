Exercise 29.

Derive the Norton equivalent circuit between nodes a and b in the circuit shown in Figure 9.6.102. The proportionality constant of the VCCS has a unit of A/V. The source voltage is equal to \(v_{AC}(t) = 3~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi60t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of the capacitors.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_{C200}} \amp= \frac{1}{j(2\pi 60)(200 \times 10^{-6})}\\ \amp= -j13.2629~\Omega\\ \vec{Z_{C500}} \amp= \frac{1}{j(2\pi 60)(500 \times 10^{-6})}\\ \amp= -j5.3052~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Transform the source into a phasor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_{AC}} \amp= 3~\cos{0^\circ} + j3~\sin{0^\circ}~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 3~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 5 Ω resistor to the two capacitors. All subsequent units are V, A, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V_{AC}} - \vec{V}}{5} = \frac{\vec{V}}{-j5.3052} + \frac{\vec{V}-\vec{V_{OC}}}{-j13.2629} \end{equation*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 8 Ω resistor to the VCCS and capacitor.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V}-\vec{V_{OC}}}{-j13.2629} = 0.3\vec{V} + \frac{\vec{V_{OC}}}{8} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V}\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V_{OC}}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 + j0.2639 \amp -j0.0754 \amp 0.6\\ 0.3 - j0.0754 \amp 0.1250 + j0.0754 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0.7837 - j0.9414\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0.2456 + j2.5840 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The open-circuit voltage is equal to 0.2456+j2.5840 V.
🔗
Short terminals a and b to calculate the Norton equivalent current.
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 5 Ω resistor to the two capacitors. All subsequent units are V, A, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V_{AC}} - \vec{V}}{5} = \frac{\vec{V}}{-j5.3052} + \frac{\vec{V}}{-j13.2629} \end{equation*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the VCCS and capacitor.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V}}{-j13.2629} = 0.3\vec{V} + \vec{I_N} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V}\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(\vec{I_N}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 + j0.2639 \amp 0 \amp 0.6\\ 0.3 - j0.0754 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 1.0945 - j1.4441\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp -0.2195 + j0.5158 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The Norton equivalent current is equal to −0.2195+j0.5158 A.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the Norton equivalent resistance.
🔗
\begin{align*} Z_N \amp= \frac{0.2456 + j2.5840~\textrm{V}}{-0.2195 + j0.5158~\textrm{A}}\\ \amp= 4.0703 - j2.2082 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the value of the capacitance based on the Thévenin equivalent reactance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} C \amp= -\frac{1}{2\pi 60 (2.2082)}\\ \amp= 1.2~\textrm{mF} \end{align*}
