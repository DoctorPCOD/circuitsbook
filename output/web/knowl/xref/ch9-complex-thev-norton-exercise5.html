Exercise 30.

Derive the Norton equivalent circuit between nodes a and b in the circuit shown in Figure 9.6.106. The source voltage has a value of \(v_{AC}(t) = 10~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi 300 t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of the capacitors.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_{C20}} \amp= \frac{1}{j(2\pi 300)(20 \times 10^{-6})}\\ \amp= -j26.5258~\Omega\\ \vec{Z_{C100}} \amp= \frac{1}{j(2\pi 300)(100 \times 10^{-6})}\\ \amp= -j5.3052~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Transform the source into a phasor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_{AC}} \amp= 10~\cos{0^\circ} + j10~\sin{0^\circ}~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 10~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 6 Ω resistor to the two capacitors. All subsequent units are V, A, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V_{AC}} - \vec{V}}{6} = \frac{\vec{V}}{-j5.3052} + \frac{\vec{V}-\vec{V_{OC}}}{-j26.5258} \end{equation*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 10 Ω resistor to the 20µF capacitor.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V}-\vec{V_{OC}}}{-j26.5258} = \frac{\vec{V_{OC}-0.8\textrm{V}}}{10} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V}\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V_{OC}}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0.1667 + j0.2262 \amp -j0.0377 \amp 1.6667\\ -0.08 - j0.0377 \amp 0.1 + 0.j0377 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 4.2283 - j4.8767\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 3.8098 - j3.7436 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The open-circuit voltage is equal to 3.8098−j3.7436 V.
🔗
Short terminals a and b to calculate the Norton equivalent current.
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 6 Ω resistor to the two capacitors. All subsequent units are V, A, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V_{AC}} - \vec{V}}{6} = \frac{\vec{V}}{-j5.3052} + \frac{\vec{V}}{-j26.5258} \end{equation*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 10 Ω resistor to the 20µF capacitor.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{\vec{V}}{-j26.5258} = \frac{-0.8\textrm{V}}{10} + \vec{I_N} \end{equation*}
🔗
The two equations can be expressed in a matrix. The left column contains coefficients of \(\vec{V}\text{,}\) the middle column contains coefficients of \(\vec{I_N}\text{,}\) and the right column contains constants.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0.1667 + j0.2262 \amp 0 \amp 1.6667\\ -0.08 - j0.0377 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix is shown in reduced row echelon form below.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 3.5188 - j4.7756\\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0.4615 - j0.2494 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
The Norton equivalent current is equal to 0.4615−j0.2494 A.
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate the Norton equivalent resistance.
🔗
\begin{align*} Z_N \amp= \frac{3.8098 - j3.7436~\textrm{V}}{0.4615 - j0.2494~\textrm{A}}\\ \amp= 9.7815 - j2.8258 \end{align*}
🔗
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the value of the capacitance based on the Thévenin equivalent reactance of the circuit.
🔗
\begin{align*} C \amp= -\frac{1}{2\pi 300 (2.8258)}\\ \amp= 187.74~\mu\textrm{F} \end{align*}
