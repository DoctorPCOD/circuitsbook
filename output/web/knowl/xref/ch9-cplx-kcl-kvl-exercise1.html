Exercise 6.

Calculate \(v(t)\) and \(i(t)\) given the circuit diagram shown in Figure 9.6.12. The source has a value of \(v_{AC}(t) = 24~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi 60t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= 4.64~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi60t - 73.2\pi/180)\\ i(t) \amp= 58~\textrm{mA}~\cos(2\pi60t -73.2\pi/180) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Use (8.2.4) to calculate the impedance of the capacitor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_C} \amp= \frac{1}{j(2\pi60)(330\times 10^{-6})}\\ \amp= -j8.0381~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Use (8.2.5) to calculate the impedance of the inductor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{Z_L}\amp= j(2\pi60)(0.01)\\ \amp= j3.7699~\Omega \end{align*}
🔗
Transform the source into a phasor.
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{V_{AC}} \amp= 24~\cos{0^\circ} + j24~\sin{0^\circ}~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 24~\textrm{V} \end{align*}
🔗
Perform KCL at the node connecting the 40 Ω resistor, −j8.04 Ω impedance, and j3.77 Ω impedance. Then, solve for \(\vec{V}\text{.}\) The units used below are V, A, and Ω.
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{\vec{V_{AC}}-\vec{V}}{40} \amp= \frac{\vec{V}}{-j8.04} + \frac{\vec{V}}{80+j3.77}\\ \frac{24}{40} \amp= \frac{\vec{V}}{40} + \frac{\vec{V}}{-j8.04} + \frac{\vec{V}}{80+j3.77}\\ \amp= \vec{V}\left(\frac{1}{40} + \frac{1}{-j8.04} + \frac{1}{80+j3.77}\right)\\ \vec{V} \amp= \frac{24}{40}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{40} + \frac{1}{-j8.04} + \frac{1}{80+j3.77}}\right)\\ \amp= 1.3435 - j4.4392~\textrm{V}\\ \amp= 4.6380~\textrm{V} \angle -73.1622^\circ \end{align*}
🔗
Use Ohm’s law to calculate \(\vec{I}\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \vec{I} \amp= \frac{1.3435 - j4.4392~\textrm{V}}{80~\Omega}\\ \amp= 0.0168 - j0.0555~\textrm{A}\\ \amp= 16.7933 - j55.4899~\textrm{mA}\\ \amp= 57.9753~\textrm{mA} \angle -73.1622^\circ \end{align*}
🔗
Convert both quantities to time-varying form.
🔗
\begin{align*} v(t) \amp= 4.64~\textrm{V}~\cos(2\pi60t - 73.2\pi/180)\\ i(t) \amp= 58~\textrm{mA}~\cos(2\pi60t -73.2\pi/180) \end{align*}
